12 chapters

C2 uses metaphors where every other level uses translations

Across the other 229 chapters, titleEn is a plain English rendering of the German title. In C2, twelve chapters put the story metaphor there instead — Ch12 "Where should I point the camera?", Ch13 "A network of roads, not one highway", Ch14 "Base camp before the summit", Ch15 "A backpack instead of a suitcase", Ch17 "A solar system of orbiting modifiers", Ch18 "The exact shade of blue", Ch19 "Every adjective is a magnet", Ch20 "Conduct the whole orchestra", Ch24 "Design the whole city", Ch26 "A video versus a photograph…", Ch29 "Climb the whole mountain…", and Ch10 "Discourse markers as train couplings".

These read well as headlines but behave badly as titles: they appear in nav cards, level maps and next-chapter links, where the learner needs to know the topic. Five of them already duplicate the chapter's own story.headline verbatim. My recommendation is to move the metaphor into headline and give titleEn a plain translation, as C2 Ch11, 16, 21–23, 25, 27–28 already do. Say the word and I'll draft the twelve.

B2 · 4 checkpoints

B2 checkpoints 14, 26, 36 and 43 declare a vocabulary section, but their vocab array is empty — the words live in vocabularyReview instead. Unless the renderer maps that key for checkpoints, the section and its nav tick render blank. Two ways out: point those sections at vocabularyReview, or drop the section from the checkpoint layout. A1 Ch35 / Ch47

Two checkpoints share the same rule text

Goethe Mini 4 and Goethe A1 Final carry an identical golden rule and an identical memory trick for connectors, word for word. A learner meeting the final exam should get the rule restated from a different angle, not replayed.

Related gap in the same two chapters: several grammar blocks have empty goldenRule and memoryTrick — A1 Ch47 blocks 2, 4 and 5, and A1 Ch35 block 4.

22 chapters

A story speaker who is never introduced

From A1 Ch15 through A2 Ch20, one dialogue partner is named in the transcript but never in the scene text or intro, so they arrive unannounced. Timo accounts for eleven of them, and A1 Ch15 and Ch41 both use the generic Kellner. A one-clause addition to each scene line fixes it; I held off because it is authored prose.

Brand rule

Emoji in the resources list, curriculum-wide

Every chapter's resources entries use emoji icons (📚 📝 📖). The project rule is no cheerful emoji in the UI, so either these are a deliberate exception or all 258 files want a swap to the icon set. Separately, 13 chapters use ⚠️ ✓ ✗ inside grammar notes and tables — those carry real meaning, and I would keep them if you agree.

Low priority

110 repeated quiz stems

Question wordings recur across chapters — "Which sentence is correct?", "Where does the participle go?", "Which closing is FORMAL?" — sometimes only a chapter or two apart (A1 Ch29 → Ch31, Ch34 → Ch35, Ch40 → Ch43). The options differ, so nothing is broken; it just reads as boilerplate. Worth a rewrite pass only if you want it.

Method note

On the prerequisite scan

I also ran every story dialogue against the teaching order for sixteen grammar features, looking for constructions used before they are taught. It produced 42 hits, and triage found almost all of them to be detector artefacts: , das ist reading as a relative clause when it is a demonstrative, polite Ihr counted as dative, mich geweckt counted as reflexive.

Two patterns are real but defensible. Accusative pronouns appear in A1 Ch4–Ch7 (Verstehst du mich?) before Akkusativ is taught in Ch12, and attributive adjectives appear as fixed chunks in A1 Ch10 and Ch47 (eine gute Methode, ein gutes Ergebnis) long before declension in A2 Ch26. Both are standard practice as memorised phrases. Flagging rather than fixing, in case you want them swapped for chunks that are fully in-scope.
