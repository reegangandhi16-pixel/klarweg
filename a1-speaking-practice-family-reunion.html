Three spoken skills to practise first

This page trains three small speaking skills: spelling your name, saying numbers and agreeing an appointment. They come up in the Goethe A1 speaking test, where Teil 1 ends with spelling a word and saying a number back. You also need them in your first weeks in Germany, on the phone and at offices. For who needs A1, which exams count and what they cost, read the A1 German for a spouse visa guide.

The German letters Indian learners mix up

Most letters sound close to English. These eight do not. Say each one aloud three times, then spell your own first name and surname.

If a letter is unclear on the phone, add a word: V wie Viktor, W wie Wilhelm. To hear every letter, use the free A1 Chapter 1: the alphabet.

Check yourself: eight quick questions

Choose an answer for each question, then check. Nothing is saved.

1. Your surname is Verma. How do you say the first letter in German?

German V is called Vau. We is the name of W, which is easy to confuse.

2. Which letter is called “Jott”?

J is Jott. G is Ge (“gay”) and Y is Ypsilon.

3. Someone reads out the last two digits of a phone number as “fünfundvierzig”. Which digits are they?

German says the unit first: fünf-und-vierzig is five-and-forty, 45. Hindi पैंतालीस also starts from the five.

4. What is siebzehn?

-zehn is the teens: siebzehn = 17. Seventy is siebzig.

5. “Der Termin ist am Dienstag um halb zehn.” When is the appointment?

halb zehn means half an hour before ten: 9:30. Dienstag is Tuesday; Thursday is Donnerstag.

6. “Wir sehen uns am dritten März.” Which date is it?

am dritten is “on the third”. März is March; May is Mai.

7. Which question asks for your date of birth?

Wann asks “when”. Wie alt asks your age and Wo wohnen Sie? asks where you live.

8. You did not catch the street name. What is the polite thing to say?

Asking someone to spell (buchstabieren) or repeat (wiederholen) is normal, polite German. Was? sounds abrupt, and Ich nicht verstehen has no verb in the right form: Ich verstehe das nicht.

Speak it: four short turns with a partner

Use made-up details if you practise in a public group. Say each turn before you open the model.

  1. Introduce a fictional person, Mira Patel, who lives in Pune.
  2. Spell the surname and ask whether the listener has it right.
  3. Confirm an appointment on Tuesday at 9:30 and ask where to go.
  4. Ask the other person to say the house number again, slowly.
Compare your four turns with a model

Ich heiße Mira Patel. Ich wohne in Pune.

Patel: P, A, T, E, L. Haben Sie das richtig?

Der Termin ist am Dienstag um halb zehn, richtig? Wohin soll ich kommen?

Können Sie die Hausnummer bitte noch einmal langsam sagen?

Then swap roles: your partner changes the day, time and house number, and you write down what you hear. You are ready to move on when you get every detail right twice in a row. For the full exam format of all three speaking parts, see Goethe A1 Sprechen examples.
