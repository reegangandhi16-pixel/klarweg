A1 to B2 German Course Duration

Plan German study from A1 to B2 with weekly study-hour scenarios, level checkpoints and an exam buffer.

Explore the A1–B2 learning path

Plan four level transitions

Write a separate study budget for A1, A2, B1 and B2. Hours often grow as the work shifts from short exchanges to longer texts, writing feedback and sustained conversation. If you already know some German, check the starting level before budgeting for all four.

LevelExample planning allocationProgress checkpoint
A160 hoursIntroduce yourself and handle a simple request.
A290 hoursChange a booking and explain a routine or plan.
B1130 hoursSummarise a short text and give a supported opinion.
B2180 hoursUnderstand a longer argument and respond with reasons.

The 460-hour total is an illustrative learner budget. It is not a course duration, a minimum requirement or a guarantee of B2 readiness.

Turn your budget into a calendar

At eight hours weekly, this example takes roughly 58 study weeks. At twelve hours, it takes roughly 39. Add time for breaks, switching level or provider, feedback, and any exam schedule that matters to your deadline.

Work backwards from an application date

First verify which certificate is accepted and the date it must be received. Put any result-delivery time and available exam dates on your calendar. Work backwards from there with a level checkpoint after each stage. Replan if a mock or teacher assessment shows a gap.
