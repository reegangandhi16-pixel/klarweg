########## a1-1 — Das Alphabet & Aussprache (The Alphabet & Pronunciation) · phase A1 · Phase 1 · 35min
DESC: Before words, the sounds. Learn the 26 letters, the four extra signs (ä ö ü ß), and the handful of rules that make German read exactly the way it is written.
SECTIONS: overview,story,vocabulary,grammar,reading,listening,speaking,writing,exercises,quiz,summary,resources
STORY scene: Deutschkurs, Tag 1 — Berlin | headline: The first day.
STORY intro: Frau Weber's very first German lesson in Berlin. She welcomes new student Rohan — and the first thing he learns is how to spell his own name, letter by letter, and where those two little dots come from.
  D1 Frau Weber: Herzlich willkommen! Ich bin Frau Weber.  [EN] A warm welcome! I am Frau Weber.  [HI] Dil se swaagat! Main Frau Weber hoon.
  D2 Rohan: Guten Tag! Ich heiße Rohan.  [EN] Hello! My name is Rohan.  [HI] Namaste! Mera naam Rohan hai.
  D3 Frau Weber: Schön! Wie ist dein Name? Buchstabier bitte.  [EN] Lovely! What is your name? Please spell it.  [HI] Badhiya! Tumhaara naam kya hai? Kripya spell karo.
  D4 Rohan: Gern : R – O – H – A – N.  [EN] Gladly: R – O – H – A – N.  [HI] Khushi se: R – O – H – A – N.
  D5 Frau Weber: Sehr gut! Und mein Name? Weber, mit W.  [EN] Very good! And my name? Weber, with a W.  [HI] Bahut achha! Aur mera naam? Weber, W ke saath.
  D6 Rohan: Mit W? Aber das klingt wie ein V!  [EN] With a W? But that sounds like a V!  [HI] W ke saath? Par yeh to V jaisa lagta hai!
  D7 Frau Weber: Genau. Und das Z? Das klingt wie ts.  [EN] Exactly. And the Z? That sounds like "ts".  [HI] Bilkul. Aur Z? Yeh "ts" jaisa lagta hai.
  D8 Rohan: Und die Punkte hier — ä, ö, ü?  [EN] And the dots here — ä, ö, ü?  [HI] Aur ye bindu yahaan — ä, ö, ü?
  D9 Frau Weber: Das sind Umlaute. Wir üben zusammen!  [EN] Those are umlauts. We’ll practise together!  [HI] Ye umlaut hain. Hum saath abhyaas karenge!
VOCAB (12): Apfel=apple | Buch=book | Eis=ice cream | Hund=dog | Jahr=year | Käse=cheese | Schule=school | Vater=father | Wasser=water | Zeit=time | Mädchen=girl | Straße=street
GRAMMAR 1: The German alphabet — 26 letters
   golden: German is written the way it is spoken. Learn the letters once — then you can read almost anything; only eight spellings need learning by hand.
   trick: J-V-W = "yot–fow–vay". Teen naam, ek saans mein. Baaki alphabet English jaisa hi hai.
   HINGLISH: German bilkul jaise likha hai waise hi bola jaata hai — isliye reading aasaan hai. Bas har letter ka naam yaad karo, kyunki apna naam aur email spell karne mein yahi kaam aayega. Teen letters dhyaan se: J = "yot", V = "fow", W = "vay".
   body: Spelling out loud has its own verb: buchstabieren. Your name, your email, your street — you will spell all of them in your first week in Germany.
   body: And the letters are worth the effort for a second reason: German spells what it says. Learn the sound of each letter and you can read almost any German word correctly on the first try, without a pronunciation guide.
GRAMMAR 2: Spelling out loud: "A wie Apfel"
   trick: "wie" = "jaisa". Letter + wie + word. Bas.
   HINGLISH: Phone par sirf letter ka naam bolne se confusion hoti hai (B aur P, D aur T ek jaise sunte hain). Isliye Germans letter ke saath ek word bolte hain: A wie Apfel = "A jaisa Apfel mein". Koi official list nahi hai — jo word aata ho, wahi bol do.
   body: You will hear this every single time you give your name or address over the phone, and you are expected to do it back. There is no fixed official list you must memorise — any well-known word works, so use the vocabulary you already have.
GRAMMAR 3: The umlauts (ä ö ü) and the ß
   golden: The dots are part of the letter, not an accent you may leave off. If you cannot type them, write ae oe ue ss — never bare a o u s.
   trick: Umlaut ka formula: honth pehle wale vowel ke, aawaaz "e" family ki — ö = honth O + "e", ü = honth U + "ee", ä = seedha "e". Aur ß vs ss: lamba vowel → ß, chhota vowel → ss.
   HINGLISH: Char extra signs hain: ä ö ü (do dots = umlaut) aur ß (sharp "ss"). ö aur ü ka trick: honth "o"/"u" ke shape mein rakho, par andar se "e"/"ee" bolo. Dots optional nahi hain — Mutter (maa) aur Mütter (maayein) do alag words hain. Type na kar pao to ae oe ue ss likho.
   body: These are not decoration. Umlaut dots change the word: Mutter is "mother", Mütter is "mothers". Drop the dots and you have said something else.
GRAMMAR 4: Eight sound rules beginners always mix up
   golden: Two moves cover almost all of it: in ei/ie say the second vowel, and at the start of a word the s-family softens — s = "z", st = "sht", sp = "shp".
   trick: ei/ie — doosra vowel bolo. v aur w ek step aage khisak jaate hain: v se f, w se v. Aur shuru mein s soft ho jaata hai: s se z, st se sht.
   HINGLISH: Ye rules yaad rakhne hi padenge, par logic simple hai: German mein ek spelling aksar ek hi tarah boli jaati hai — English ki tarah har word ka guess nahi karna padta. ei = "eye" (Eis = ice), ie = "ee" (Liebe = LEE-buh), z = "ts" (Zeit = TSAIT), sch = English "sh" (Schule = SHOO-luh), v = "f" (Vater = FAH-ter), w = "v" (Wasser = VA-ser), aur shuru ka s = "z" (sagen = ZAH-gen), st- = "sht" (Straße = SHTRAH-suh). Sirf v ka ek exception hai: international words mein v = "v" hi rehta hai (Video, Vase).
   body: Seven of the eight are completely regular — the spelling always makes the same sound. Only v has a second setting, and it is easy to spot.

########## a1-4 — Hallo! (Greetings & Introductions) · phase A1 · Phase 1 · 40min
DESC: Your first real conversation. Greet by the time of day, say who you are and where you are from, and choose the right register — du or Sie — so you sound polite from the very first word.
SECTIONS: overview,story,vocabulary,grammar,reading,listening,speaking,writing,exercises,quiz,summary,resources
STORY scene: Anmeldung an der Sprachschule — Berlin | headline: The right hello.
STORY intro: David arrives at his Berlin language school and meets the receptionist, Frau Klein. A first, formal conversation — Sie, Herr and Frau — and he learns exactly when Germans switch to the friendly du.
  D1 Frau Klein: Guten Tag! Herzlich willkommen!  [EN] Good day! A warm welcome!  [HI] Namaskar! Hardik swaagat!
  D2 David: Guten Tag! Ich heiße David Sharma.  [EN] Good day! My name is David Sharma.  [HI] Namaskar! Mera naam David Sharma hai.
  D3 Frau Klein: Freut mich, Herr Sharma. Woher kommen Sie?  [EN] Pleased to meet you, Mr Sharma. Where are you from?  [HI] Aap se milkar khushi hui, Herr Sharma. Aap kahaan se hain?
  D4 David: Ich komme aus Indien. Jetzt wohne ich in Berlin.  [EN] I come from India. Now I live in Berlin.  [HI] Main Bharat se hoon. Ab main Berlin mein rehta hoon.
  D5 Frau Klein: Wunderbar! Ihr Deutsch ist schon gut.  [EN] Wonderful! Your German is already good.  [HI] Shaandaar! Aapka German pehle se achha hai.
  D6 David: Danke schön! Und ich? Sie oder du?  [EN] Thank you! And me? "Sie" or "du"?  [HI] Dhanyavaad! Aur main? "Sie" ya "du"?
  D7 Frau Klein: Ich bin die Lehrerin — bitte Sie. Freunde sind du.  [EN] I am the teacher — please "Sie". Friends are "du".  [HI] Main teacher hoon — kripya "Sie". Dost "du" hain.
  D8 David: Ah! Hallo und du sind informell, oder?  [EN] Ah! "Hallo" and "du" are informal, right?  [HI] Achha! "Hallo" aur "du" informal hain, na?
  D9 Frau Klein: Genau! Auf Wiedersehen, Herr Sharma.  [EN] Exactly! Goodbye, Mr Sharma.  [HI] Bilkul! Alvida, Herr Sharma.
  D10 David: Auf Wiedersehen, Frau Klein! Bis bald!  [EN] Goodbye, Mrs Klein! See you soon.  [HI] Alvida, Frau Klein! Jald milte hain.

GRAMMAR 1: Greetings — by the time of day
   golden: Morning · day · evening → Guten Morgen · Guten Tag · Guten Abend. Unsure of the hour? Guten Tag.
   trick: Subah–din–shaam = Morgen–Tag–Abend. Aur Nacht wala sirf bistar ke liye.
   HINGLISH: German mein greeting ghadi dekh kar chunte hain — koi ek "hello" nahi jo har waqt polite bhi ho. Hallo kabhi bhi chalega, par woh dostana hai, formal nahi. Guten Morgen (subah), Guten Tag (din — sabse safe), Guten Abend (shaam). Gute Nacht sirf sone jaate waqt — yeh hello nahi hai. Aur dhyaan do: Gute Nacht par Guten Tag — ending badal jaati hai. Wajah baad ke chapter mein aayegi; abhi chaaron ko poore phrase ki tarah yaad rakho.
   body: Hallo is the exception: it works almost anytime and with almost anyone, but it stays friendly rather than formal. The "Guten …" greetings are the polite set.
   body: Regional hellos exist too, and Germans use them constantly: Moin in the north, Grüß Gott in the south and Austria, Grüezi in Switzerland. You do not have to say them, but you should recognise them.
GRAMMAR 2: Saying goodbye
   golden: Formal = Auf Wiedersehen. Friendly = Tschüs and the bis … family.
   trick: bis = "tak". Jo bhi time word peeche lagao, goodbye ban jaata hai.
   HINGLISH: Goodbye bhi formal/informal hota hai, aur yeh galat hona greeting se zyada notice hota hai — kyunki yeh aakhri cheez hai jo saamne wale ne suna. Auf Wiedersehen = formal alvida. Tschüs, Ciao = casual. Bis … ka matlab "… tak" hai, isliye yeh poora family time ka vaada hai: bis bald (jald tak), bis später (baad tak), bis morgen (kal tak) — matlab tum khud naye bana sakte ho.
   body: Auf Wiedersehen is the full, formal goodbye. Everything else on this list is friendly.
GRAMMAR 3: Introduce yourself — the four questions
   golden: Name → Ich heiße … · Origin → Ich komme aus … · Age → Ich bin … Jahre alt.
   trick: Naam ke liye heiße, desh ke liye komme aus, umar ke liye bin … Jahre alt. Teen kaam, teen fixed phrase.
   HINGLISH: Pehli baat-cheet chaar sawaal-jawaab par chalti hai, aur inhe jodi mein seekhna chahiye — akela sentence yaad karne se tum sirf jawaab de paoge, sawaal nahi. Wie heißt du? → Ich heiße … (naam ke liye alag verb hai). Woher kommst du? → Ich komme aus … (desh ya shehar, dono ke liye aus). Wie alt bist du? → Ich bin … Jahre alt (Chapter 3 wala pattern). Aur agar rehte kahin aur ho: Ich wohne in Berlin.
   body: Notice that two of the four answers use Ich bin and two use their own verb. That is not random — bin is for identity and age, heiße is specifically for names, and komme aus is specifically for origin.
GRAMMAR 4: Formal vs informal — du or Sie
   golden: du → verb ends in -st. Sie → verb ends in -en, and Sie is always capitalised.
   trick: du = -st, Sie = -en, ich = -e. Teen ending, teen log. Aur Sie hamesha capital S. (Stem mein pehle se s/ß/z ho to du ka ending sirf -t: du heißt.)
   HINGLISH: German mein "you" ke do roop hain, aur yeh choice tumhari politeness ka sabse saaf signal hai. Doston, ghar walon aur bacchon ke saath du. Baaki sab ke saath Sie + Herr/Frau + surname. Sirf shabd nahi badalta — verb ka ending bhi badalta hai: du ke saath -st (du kommst), Sie ke saath -en (Sie kommen), aur ich ke saath -e (ich komme). Aur formal "aapka" = Ihr (Ihr Name), informal = dein. Ek chhoti baat: agar verb ka stem pehle se s/ß/z par khatam hota hai, to du ka ending sirf -t ban jaata hai — du heißt, kyunki "heißst" bola hi nahi jaa sakta. Sabse zaruri baat: ek conversation mein ek hi register — du aur Sie mix karna sabse zyada notice hota hai. Doubt ho to Sie; zyada polite hona kabhi galat nahi.
   body: And the choice does more than swap one word: the verb ending changes with it. That is why the same question looks different in the two columns below.
GRAMMAR 5: Mistakes Hindi & English speakers make
   trick: Chaar check: Sie chahiye tha? Register same raha? Nacht sirf sote waqt? Desh ke saath aus?
   HINGLISH: Chaar galtiyan hoti hain, aur inme se sirf ek vocabulary ki hai — baaki teen register ki hain, jahan German ki asli politeness rehti hai. Pehli, Herr ya Frau ke saath surname aaye to Sie aur -en ending aani chahiye. Doosri, ek hi conversation mein register badalna — formal shuru karke informal khatam karna sabse zyada ajeeb lagta hai. Teesri, Gute Nacht aate waqt nahi bolte; shaam ko Guten Abend aata hai. Aur chauthi, desh ke liye aus lagta hai, "von" nahi.
   body: Each row is a rule from this chapter, seen from the wrong side.

GRAMMAR 1: The personal pronouns (Personalpronomen)
   golden: Choose the pronoun first, then the verb follows it. Never the other way round.
   trick: Verb hi sie ka matlab kholta hai: ist = akeli (vah), sind = kai (ve/aap).
   HINGLISH: Personalpronomen = wo chhota word jo subject ki jagah aata hai. Aath yaad karo, aur isi order mein: ich (main), du (tum), er/sie/es (vah), wir (hum), ihr (tum sab), sie (ve), Sie (aap). Aage ke saare verb tables isi order mein aayenge, isliye order khud hi yaad rakhne mein madad karta hai. Teen roop ek jaise dikhte hain — sie=vah (stri), sie=ve, Sie=aap — par guess karne ki zarurat nahi: verb bata deta hai. sie ist = vah hai; sie sind = ve hain / aap hain.
   body: Learn them as one block, in this order. Every verb table you meet from now on — in this chapter and every chapter after it — is printed in exactly this order, so the order itself becomes a memory aid.
GRAMMAR 2: The verb "sein" (to be)
   golden: Six forms, three of them the same word: bin · bist · ist · sind · seid · sind.
   trick: bin–bist sirf main aur tum ke liye. Uske baad sab s se: ist · sind · seid · sind.
   HINGLISH: sein (hona) German ka sabse zyada use hone wala verb hai, aur bilkul irregular hai — forms infinitive jaisi dikhti hi nahi. Par ghabrane ki baat nahi: sirf chhe chhote shabd hain, aur teen pronoun ek hi shabd share karte hain. Do group bana lo: b- wale singular aur personal hain (bin, bist), aur s- wale baaki sab (ist, sind, seid, sind). wir, sie aur Sie — teeno sind, matlab ek form se aadhi table cover ho gayi. Galti se bhi "ich bist" ya "du bin" nahi.
   body: You need this verb for almost everything in this chapter: who you are, where you are, how old you are, what you are.
GRAMMAR 3: Other verbs: what er, sie and es do
   golden: ich -e · du -st · er/sie/es -t.
   trick: -e, -st, -t — main, tum, vah. Teen ending, teen log.
   HINGLISH: Apne baare mein bol liya, ab kisi aur ke baare mein. Aam verbs mein — sein ko chhod kar — er/sie/es ke saath sirf -t lagta hai: sie kommt, er wohnt, sie heißt. Poora pattern: ich -e, du -st, er/sie/es -t. Ek dilchasp baat: heißen mein du heißt aur sie heißt ek jaise ban jaate hain — isliye pronoun girana kabhi possible nahi.

GRAMMAR 5: Word order: the verb is always second

   trick: Verb ki seat number 2 reserved hai. Pehli seat par kuch bhi baithe, verb apni seat nahi chhodta.

########## a1-9 — Verben im Alltag (Everyday Verbs) · phase A1 · Phase 2 · 45min
DESC: Verbs are the engine of every German sentence. Learn the everyday ones — arbeiten, lernen, machen, spielen, kochen — and the one regular pattern of present-tense endings that conjugates almost all of them across ich, du, er, wir, ihr, sie.

GRAMMAR 1: What is a verb? The infinitive
   golden: Cut -en off the infinitive and you have the stem. Everything you ever do to a German verb happens after that stem.
   trick: Infinitiv = stem + -en. Ulta karo: -en hatao, stem mil gaya.
   HINGLISH: Verb action word hai — sentence ka engine. Dictionary wala base form = Infinitiv, jo aksar -en par khatam hota hai (lernen, machen). -en hatao to bachta hai stem (lern-, mach-). Regular verbs mein stem nahi badalta, sirf ending badalti hai.
   body: A verb (Verb) is the action word — the engine of the sentence. Its base form, the one you find in a dictionary, is the Infinitiv.
   body: Almost every German infinitive ends in -en (a few in just -n). Cut that off and what is left is the stem — the part you build on.
GRAMMAR 2: The present-tense endings
   golden: ich -e · du -st · er/sie/es -t · wir -en · ihr -t · sie/Sie -en.
   trick: Do endings do baar aate hain: -en (wir, sie/Sie) aur -t (er, ihr). Matlab chhe forms mein sirf chaar shabd yaad karne hain.
   HINGLISH: Verb use karne ke liye subject ke hisaab se ending lagao: ich -e, du -st, er/sie/es -t, wir -en, ihr -t, sie/Sie -en. Ek hi set lagbhag har regular verb par chalta hai. Dhyaan: wir aur sie/Sie infinitive jaise hi (-en) hain.
   body: To use a verb, add the ending that matches the subject to the stem. This single set of endings works for almost every regular verb — learn it once.
GRAMMAR 3: Spelling helper: stems ending in -t or -d
   golden: Stem ends in -t or -d? Slip an -e- in before the du, er and ihr endings.
   trick: Do consonant takra rahe hain? Beech mein ek -e- daal do.
   HINGLISH: Agar stem -t ya -d par khatam ho (jaise arbeit-), to du/er/ihr mein ek extra -e- aata hai: du arbeitest, er arbeitet. Warna "arbeitst" bola hi nahi jaata. Yeh rule sirf -t/-d wale stems par.
   body: If the stem already ends in -t or -d, the du, er/sie/es and ihr forms add an extra -e- so you can actually pronounce them.
GRAMMAR 4: Word order: verb in second position
   golden: Whatever fills slot 1, the verb is slot 2 — and the subject moves out of the way if it has to.
   trick: Verb ki seat number 2 reserved hai. Time word aage aaya to subject peeche khisak jaata hai.
   HINGLISH: Normal statement (Hauptsatz) mein verb doosri position par hota hai (Chapter 5 wala rule). Agar time word pehle aaye (morgen, am Wochenende), to verb phir bhi doosre, aur subject uske peeche: Morgen arbeite ich — "Morgen ich arbeite" nahi.
   body: In a statement the conjugated verb stays in second position — the same rule from Chapter 5. If a time word like morgen or am Wochenende comes first, the verb still sits second and the subject jumps behind it.

Doosri, jin stems ke end mein -t ya -d hai, unme extra -e- bhool jaana: du arbeitest. Teesri, time word aage hone par verb ko doosri jagah par na rakhna: Morgen arbeite ich. ########## a1-12 — Akkusativ (The Accusative Case) · phase A1 · Phase 2 · 45min
DESC: The accusative is the case of the direct object — the thing a verb acts on. The good news: only one thing changes. Masculine der becomes den (and ein becomes einen); die, das and plural die stay exactly the same.
SECTIONS: overview,story,vocabulary,grammar,reading,listening,speaking,writing,exercises,quiz,summary,resources
STORY scene: Im Geschäft — Berlin | headline: In the shop.
STORY intro: Tom and Lisa go shopping after class. Tom is looking for a few things — and every object he wants becomes a direct object in the accusative.
  D1 Tom: Ich suche den Schlüssel.  [EN] I am looking for the key.  [HI] Main chaabi dhoondh raha hoon.
  D2 Lisa: Und ich brauche einen Stift und ein Heft.  [EN] And I need a pen and an exercise book.  [HI] Aur mujhe ek pen aur ek copy chahiye.
  D3 Tom: Hast du den Stift?  [EN] Do you have the pen?  [HI] Kya tumhaare paas pen hai?
  D4 Lisa: Ja! Und ich habe auch ein Buch.  [EN] Yes! And I also have a book.  [HI] Haan! Aur mere paas kitaab bhi hai.
  D5 Tom: Das Geschäft hat alles!  [EN] The shop has everything!  [HI] Is dukaan mein sab kuch hai!
  D6 Lisa: Ja! Und ich finde alles hier.  [EN] Yes! And I find everything here.  [HI] Haan! Aur mujhe yahaan sab kuch mil jaata hai.
  D7 Tom: Ich brauche die Karte für Ben.  [EN] I need the card for Ben.  [HI] Mujhe Ben ke liye ye card chahiye.
  D8 Lisa: Schön! Wir haben alles. Wir sind gut!  [EN] Lovely! We have everything. We are good!  [HI] Badhiya! Hamaare paas sab kuch hai. Hum theek hain!

GRAMMAR 1: Subject, verb, object
   golden: Ask Wen? or Was? after the verb. Whatever answers is your accusative object.
   trick: Verb ke baad Wen? poochho — jo jawaab aaye, wahi Akkusativ.
   HINGLISH: Zyadatar sentence mein teen hisse: subject (kaun — Nominativ), verb (action), aur object (kis par — Akkusativ). Object dhoondhne ke liye verb ke baad Wen? (kisko) ya Was? (kya) poochho. Subject action karta hai, aur object us action ko receive karta hai.
   body: Most sentences have three parts: who does it (the subject, in the Nominativ), the action (the verb), and what it is done to (the direct object, in the Akkusativ).
   body: Find the object by asking Wen? (whom?) for a person or Was? (what?) for a thing, after the verb.
GRAMMAR 2: The one change: der → den
   golden: Only the masculine changes: der → den, ein → einen. Feminine, neuter and plural do not move at all.
   trick: Chaar gender mein se teen bilkul same rehte hain. Bas masculine par dhyaan do: der → den.
   HINGLISH: Poora Akkusativ ek line mein: sirf masculine article badalta hai — der → den (den Mann). Feminine (die), neuter (das), plural (die) bilkul same rehte hain. Indefinite bhi: ein → einen (einen Stift); eine/ein same. Aur kein → keinen (Chapter 11).
   body: Here is the whole accusative in one line: only the masculine article changes. Feminine, neuter and plural look exactly the same as the nominative.
GRAMMAR 3: Verbs that take the accusative
   golden: If the verb can be followed by "…what?", it takes an accusative object.
   trick: Verb aur object ko hamesha ek saath practice karo — einen Stift haben, den Schlüssel suchen — taaki case sochna na pade.
   HINGLISH: Bahut saare rozmarra verbs ke baad object Akkusativ mein aata hai: haben, sehen, suchen, finden, kaufen, brauchen, essen, trinken, lesen. Verb ko hamesha "… + object" ke saath practice karo, taaki case sochne ki zarurat na pade.
   body: Many everyday verbs are followed by a direct object in the accusative. Learn the verb together with "… + object" so the case comes automatically.
GRAMMAR 4: Mistakes Hindi & English speakers make
   HINGLISH: Char galtiyan common hain. Agar object masculine hai to den aayega: Ich sehe den Mann. ein bhi masculine object ke saath einen ban jaata hai: einen Stift. Feminine aur neuter ko badalna nahi hai — die Karte aur das Buch waise hi rehte hain. Yaad rakho ki Akkusativ mein sirf masculine badalta hai, aur yahi poora rule hai.
   body: Each row is a rule from this chapter, seen from the wrong side.
