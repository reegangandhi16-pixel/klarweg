Days 1–7: sounds and first conversations

  1. Listen to five alphabet sounds and repeat them.
  2. Spell your name and city aloud.
  3. Practise saying your name with Ich heiße …
  4. Say which city you live in and what language you speak.
  5. Count aloud from one to twenty.
  6. Ask a partner their name and city.
  7. Record a 30-second introduction from memory.

Checkpoint: Can someone identify your name, city and language from the recording?

Days 8–14: ask Time, Place and Price

  1. Say three prices aloud.
  2. Ask what an item costs.
  3. Practise three clock times.
  4. Ask when a bus leaves.
  5. Read a shop opening-hours sign.
  6. Order one item in a role-play and ask for the total.
  7. Repeat the exchange with a new price and time.

Checkpoint: Give a partner one price and time. Ask them to repeat both.

Days 15–21: make and change a plan

  1. Say which days you are free.
  2. Write an invitation with a place and time.
  3. Read a short meeting note and identify the date.
  4. Ask whether Saturday at ten works.
  5. Say you cannot come and offer another day.
  6. Listen to a changed time without reading the transcript.
  7. Write a three-line reply confirming the new plan.

Checkpoint: Check that your reply gives the final day, time and place.

Days 22–30: use German with fewer prompts

  1. Review five sentences you still need to read.
  2. Ask someone to repeat a sentence more slowly.
  3. Listen for two details in a short announcement.
  4. Read an invitation and write a simple reply.
  5. Repeat your first introduction with a new detail.
  6. Role-play a shop or station exchange.
  7. Do one short task in each of the four skills.
  8. Review the weakest skill with a changed example.
  9. Record what you can do without notes and choose your next chapter.
Catch up without doubling tomorrow’s work

Use your next review day to finish the one missed task that unlocks later practice. If you missed times, for example, practise three times before writing the appointment reply. Treat these 30 days as a foundation, rather than a guarantee of A1 completion.
