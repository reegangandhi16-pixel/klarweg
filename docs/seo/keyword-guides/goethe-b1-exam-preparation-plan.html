What you are preparing for

Goethe-Zertifikat B1 has four modules. Each one is marked out of 100 and passed on its own with 60 points, so a strong reading score cannot rescue a weak speaking score. You can book all four together or one at a time.

The four-week plan

This plan is for the last month, when you have finished B1 grammar. If you are earlier than that, use the twelve-week plan in the B1 exam guide first. Plan five sessions a week of 60–90 minutes.

WeekSessionsMove on when
1: DiagnoseDay 1: official practice set, Lesen and Hören, timed. Day 2: Schreiben, timed. Day 3: record Sprechen Teil 2 on your phone. Days 4–5: mark everything with the answer key and list your mistakes by task type.You know your score per module and your two weakest task types.
2: Weakest moduleTwo Lesen or Hören parts a day from your weak list. Read the questions before the audio and underline one key word in each. One Schreiben task every other day; check that every point is covered before you check grammar.Your weak task types reach at least 6 of 10 correct.
3: Speaking and writingSprechen Teil 1 with a partner: plan something, react, decide. Teil 2: one presentation a day using the five slides, with one Indian example. Write the three Schreiben tasks within 20, 25 and 15 minutes.You can present for three minutes without reading full sentences.
4: RehearseDay 1: a second full set, all four modules, timed. Days 2–3: rework only the mistakes. Day 4: speaking with a partner. Day 5: light review; confirm the exam time, centre and ID.Every module scores 60 or more in the rehearsal. If one does not, consider booking that module on a later date.

Check yourself: Lesen Teil 4 opinions

In Lesen Teil 4 you decide whether each person is for an idea. People rarely say it directly. For each comment below, decide whether the writer supports the idea of a four-day working week.

1. „Natürlich gibt es Probleme, trotzdem wäre das für Familien eine große Hilfe.“

The opinion comes after trotzdem (“even so”): a big help for families.

2. „Die Idee klingt gut, aber in meinem Krankenhaus wäre das unmöglich.“

The first half is polite; aber … unmöglich carries the real opinion.

3. „Ich finde das gar nicht schlecht.“

gar nicht schlecht means “not bad at all”: a positive opinion despite the word nicht.

4. „Wer soll dann am Freitag die Kunden betreuen? Daran hat wohl niemand gedacht.“

A question that points out a problem, followed by “nobody thought of that”, is criticism.

5. „Anfangs war ich skeptisch. Seit wir es in unserer Firma testen, bin ich überzeugt.“

überzeugt means “convinced”. The earlier doubt is in the past.

6. „Weniger arbeiten klingt schön. Weniger verdienen will aber keiner.“

The writer expects lower pay and says nobody wants that: against.
