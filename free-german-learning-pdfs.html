Free German learning PDFs

Official source directory · Last checked 8 November 2026

Find the right kind of PDF

A search for “free German learning PDFs” can lead to very different materials: an official word list, a sample exam, a publisher worksheet or a scan of a commercial textbook. Start with the task you need to practise and the organisation that produced the file. This directory links to official sources; it does not host copies of other publishers’ books.

A PDF is useful for annotation and printing, but it cannot supply listening practice by itself. When the task mentions a recording, download or open the matching audio from the same source. If your phone has limited storage or data, select one unit at a time and keep its instructions, audio and key together.

Choose material for your current level

If you are new to German, begin with the alphabet, pronunciation and a short course sequence before attempting an entire exam paper. The alphabet guide and free A1 starting chapter provide a route into those basics. Then use material labelled for the level you are studying.

For A2 or B1, choose texts you can mostly follow while still needing to work on particular structures. For B2 and above, add sustained reading and listening rather than relying on lists alone. Consult the A1–C2 course overview to identify the level sequence. A resource label is a guide to selection, not a placement result.

Vocabulary PDFs: build recall, not a collection

Where Goethe supplies a word list for your selected exam, use it to identify gaps rather than as a promise that memorising it guarantees a pass. Keep nouns with articles and plurals, and verbs with their common combinations. Study words inside short phrases and return to them over several days.

A manageable routine is to cover the translation, recall the meaning, then produce a sentence you understand. Separate words you recognise in reading from words you can retrieve when speaking. Do not label an unofficial collection a complete official B2 or C1 vocabulary list without checking the provider’s documentation.

Print, annotate and keep track of sources

Save the source page address beside each file, together with the level, edition and date you obtained it. Print only the section you will use, retaining the instructions and any key you need. For shared devices, a clear filename makes it easier to avoid studying the wrong version.

Turn a resource into a study session

Choose one specific outcome before opening a file: recognise a sentence pattern, retrieve a set of words or complete one task without help. Read the instructions, attempt the work, then check the key. Record the cause of each error, not only the correct answer. On the next day, try the difficult point again before looking at your notes.

Keep one main learning sequence and use other materials to solve a named problem. If you change books whenever an exercise becomes difficult, you lose the chance to revisit and consolidate. Conversely, if most instructions and examples remain inaccessible, step back to an easier level rather than collecting more advanced files.

Common download questions

Is every resource here a free complete book? No. Official practice papers, sample chapters and supplements serve different purposes. Read the provider’s label before downloading.

Can I prepare with PDFs alone? Include audio, writing feedback and speaking practice where your learning goal requires them. A printed task does not test whether you understood a recording.

Where are exam PDFs? The official German exam practice directory separates providers and links their own resources.
