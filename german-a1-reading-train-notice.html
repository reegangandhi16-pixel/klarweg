German A1 Reading: Train Notice Practice

Use this guide to find a destination, time and changed track in a short notice. Work through original German examples, Hindi meaning checks and exercises with explanations.

Understand this topic

Read a transport notice in two passes. First identify what kind of message it is. Then locate the details needed for the traveller’s decision. A track change does not necessarily mean a time change. Keep old and new information distinct instead of selecting the first number you see. The short notice below is original and fictional; it is not an official examination item or a real travel announcement.

Read the original passage

Original fictional text · not an official paper

Information für Reisende
Der Zug nach Bonn fährt heute um 14 Uhr ab. Er fährt nicht von Gleis zwei, sondern von Gleis vier. Bitte gehen Sie zu Gleis vier.
Die Fahrt dauert dreißig Minuten. Der Zug hält auch in Weststadt. Für Informationen zur Rückfahrt fragen Sie bitte am Schalter.
Am Bahnhof gibt es heute keinen Kaffee. Der kleine Laden ist geschlossen. Wasser können Sie im Laden gegenüber dem Bahnhof kaufen.
Vielen Dank für Ihr Verständnis.

The fictional train to Bonn departs at 14:00 from track four rather than track two. The journey takes thirty minutes and also stops at Weststadt. Return-trip information is available at the counter. A small station shop is closed; the notice mentions a shop opposite the station for water.

Try the questions before reading the English overview again. The overview is a meaning check, not the source of extra facts to add to the passage. When a detail is absent, distinguish “not stated” from “false”: those are different reading decisions. Do not infer a real timetable, service policy or general research finding from this fictional text.

Try it yourself

Write a short answer to each question and identify the sentence or phrase that supports it. Do not use a likely real-world answer if the passage says something different. The key explains why the answer follows from this particular text.

  1. Where is the train going?
  2. What is the departure time?
  3. Which track should the traveller use?
  4. Where can the traveller ask about the return trip?
  5. Is the small station shop open?

Answers and explanations

Show answers after your attempt
  1. Where is the train going?

    Bonn. The destination follows nach in the opening sentence.

  2. What is the departure time?

    14 Uhr, or 2 pm. Do not choose the thirty-minute journey duration.

  3. Which track should the traveller use?

    Track four. The notice explicitly rejects track two with nicht … sondern.

  4. Where can the traveller ask about the return trip?

    At the counter. The return journey is not fully described in this notice.

  5. Is the small station shop open?

    No. Geschlossen means closed; the other shop is opposite the station.

Use it in a new situation

Create a new notice by changing only the destination, time and old/new track. Give it to a partner who has not seen your changes. Ask the same five kinds of questions. This checks whether they retrieve information from the new text instead of remembering answers from the original. Do not use the fictional notice to plan an actual journey.

Finish by writing a short summary without adding facts from outside the passage. Reopen the source and remove any detail you cannot support. Then choose one unfamiliar expression and study its role in the sentence. This combines meaning, evidence and vocabulary work instead of turning reading into the translation of isolated words.

Questions

Should I translate the entire passage first?

Try identifying its purpose and requested details first. Use the glossary for a focused meaning check, then return to the relevant sentence.

Can I answer from general knowledge?

Base comprehension answers on this fictional passage. A plausible real-world fact does not supply evidence for something the text does not say.

Does a correct answer establish my CEFR level?

No. One short reading activity cannot certify a level or predict a full examination result. The level in the title describes the intended practice context.

What should I do after checking the key?

Locate the supporting phrase for each answer, explain any error and summarise the text in your own words. Revisit a specific unclear expression rather than rereading without a purpose.
