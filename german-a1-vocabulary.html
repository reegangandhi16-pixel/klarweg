Some words have other senses. For example, the gloss “pen / pencil” describes a common use of the general word for a writing implement; it does not identify one specific kind of pen. Check the surrounding lesson or a dictionary when a familiar word appears in a different context. A short gloss should help you begin, not replace attention to meaning.

A short vocabulary practice routine

  1. Choose a group. Start with the topic that matches your current lesson. Read the meaning and keep the article attached to the noun.
  2. Retrieve it. Cover the German column and try to recall the word. Then check the article and spelling instead of accepting a partly remembered answer as complete.
  3. Check the plural. Compare singular and plural directly. Notice which letters or endings changed rather than assuming every noun follows one pattern.
  4. Return to context. Find the word in your lesson or recording and follow the message around it. A word you can recognise on a sheet may be harder to identify in speech.
  5. Review selectively. Give the difficult entries another attempt later. Add a note about the problem: meaning, spelling, article, plural or sound.

If you use Hindi to understand the meaning, that is fine. During a listening or reading task, try to stay with the German message rather than translating every word in sequence. Use the gloss only where you need it, then close it and attempt the task again.

The beginner self-study plan and its separate weekly planner can help you put these short sessions into a routine. Neither the routine nor the sheet specifies how quickly you should finish A1.

Connect vocabulary to articles and grammar

Articles and plurals are easier to lose if you record only an English translation. Keep the full entry together and revisit it when you meet a related structure. The article and gender guide explains the grammatical system; this page gives you a small reference set to practise remembering.

For other beginner topics, use the existing numbers guide, time expressions guide and question-word guide. These are separate references with their own examples. You do not need another near-identical word list for every topic to make progress.

If you are building a notebook from a coursebook, add a page reference or lesson name beside each entry. That makes it possible to return to the recording and sentence that gave it meaning. Avoid collecting more lists when the difficulty is recalling words you have already encountered.

Questions

Is this the full German A1 vocabulary?

No. It is a 24-noun starter selection. Use the official source and a connected learning sequence for a broader reference and practice.

Does the PDF include Hindi meanings?

The PDF is German–English. Hindi glosses are provided in the web tables above.

Can I use this instead of a coursebook?

Use it as a revision aid. A noun list does not provide a complete lesson sequence, pronunciation feedback or interaction practice. The beginner books guide explains how to choose a main learning resource.
