The same-looking plural in der Schlüssel / die Schlüssel still needs the article to show the number. Compare it with der Eingang / die Eingänge, where both the article and the noun form change. Do not use one plural ending for every new noun. Learn the written pair, then use one item and two items in separate sentences.

Build useful A2 sentence patterns

Use the list to complete everyday jobs: arrange a meeting, find a stop, ask about a bill or describe a problem at home. Start with one short sentence that communicates the important detail. Add a time, place or reason only when you understand how it fits. The examples below are fictional, original learning models, not exam answers.

Original Klarweg practice

Ich habe am Donnerstag einen Termin. Können wir uns um zehn Uhr treffen?

I have an appointment on Thursday. Can we meet at ten? Notice that the dictionary form der Termin becomes einen Termin after habe in this sentence. Learn the dictionary form and the sentence form together.

Original Klarweg practice

Der Bus hat Verspätung. Ich warte an der Haltestelle.

The bus is delayed. I am waiting at the stop. Here two nouns describe one situation. Replace the transport or the place, then check whether the changed sentence still communicates a sensible message.

Original Klarweg practice

Die Heizung funktioniert nicht. Wann ist die Reparatur möglich?

The heating is not working. When is the repair possible? The first sentence states the problem, and the second asks for useful information. Avoid translating an entire Hindi paragraph before you can express these two clear points.

Practise verbs with the nouns: have an appointment, buy a ticket, pay a bill, sign a form. A noun list gives you names for things, but a sentence also needs an action and a clear relationship between its parts. For articles and sentence structure, use the German grammar syllabus alongside this page.

Use Hindi to check the intended meaning

A short Hindi gloss is a starting point. die Rechnung means बिल (bil) here, while die Quittung means रसीद (raseed). In a shop, the document stating what you owe and the confirmation that you paid serve different purposes. Practise choosing the German word from the situation, rather than treating both as an interchangeable word for paper.

Likewise, an English “appointment” is not the same thing as a job appointment in every context. In this selection, Termin refers to a scheduled meeting or time. Write a tiny context next to a new word in your notebook. If you cannot explain what the word names in that context, return to the meaning before rehearsing its spelling.

Say the German from the German spelling and a reliable sound reference. Reading the Hindi transliteration aloud will only produce the Hindi meaning. If your earlier nouns are uncertain, return to A1 vocabulary with Hindi meanings before adding another large set.

Three activities that make the words usable

First, make situation cards. On the front write “You are late because the bus has not arrived.” On the back write a short German message using a noun from the travel group. Try the card before looking at the model. Change the reason or the destination on the next attempt so the activity requires a fresh choice.

Second, practise a service exchange with a partner. One person needs information about a meeting; the other has the date and time. Ask, answer and write down what you understood. Compare notes afterwards. If the appointment time differs, identify whether the difficulty was a number, a question or a missed word rather than repeating everything without a diagnosis.

Third, organise a small picture-free recall test. Write four topic headings and retrieve two nouns under each, including the article. Then add the plural of one noun from each heading. Check against the table only after attempting retrieval. Recognition while looking at an answer is a different task from finding the word when you need it.

A manageable vocabulary review routine

Choose six entries for a study session. Read the forms and meanings, then close the page and retrieve them from a context cue. Write a sentence for two entries and read it aloud. Record the specific error: article, plural, meaning, spelling or sentence construction. Different errors need different follow-up work; rewriting the same list may not address the actual difficulty.

At the next session, test the earlier set before adding a new group. Put uncertain entries back into short situations, while allowing secure ones to appear in fresh reading or conversation. The number six is a manageable activity size, not a scientific requirement or a promise about how quickly you will reach a level.

Keep a small record with three columns: I can recognise it, I can retrieve it, and I can use it in a sentence. A word can be in the first column for a while before it belongs in the third. Ask a teacher or proficient reviewer to check new sentences, especially when the article or verb combination feels uncertain.

Questions

Is this the complete A2 word list?

No. This is a focused 24-noun practice selection. Open the linked official PDF for the provider’s broader reference, and use a structured course to develop the language around it.

Are these words exclusive to A2?

No. Common vocabulary is reused across learning stages. The label describes the intended practice context, not an exclusive classification for each word.

Should I learn the article and plural together?

Yes. Record the noun with its article and full plural, then practise the form needed in a real sentence. A singular dictionary entry alone does not show every grammatical change.

Does Roman text show German pronunciation?

No. It transliterates the Hindi meaning for readers who need that aid. Use the German spelling and reliable German audio for pronunciation.

Will memorising this table be enough to pass?

No. It is a small learning activity and cannot establish exam readiness. Use official practice and feedback across the language skills; no pass result is promised.
