Understand this topic

An opinion text can present a preference without claiming that it works for everyone. Find the main view, the evidence offered and the qualification. Watch whether the conclusion rejects the alternative or combines it with the writer’s preferred method. This original reading passage is fictional personal reasoning, not a research summary or an official examination item. Its learning labels describe practice difficulty rather than certify your level.

Read the original passage

Original fictional text · not an official paper

Papier oder Bildschirm beim Lernen?
Wenn ich einen neuen Text lese, arbeite ich zunächst lieber auf Papier. Ich kann Fragen an den Rand schreiben und sehe meine Notizen beim zweiten Lesen sofort. Vor allem bei längeren Absätzen hilft mir das, die Verbindung zwischen einer Behauptung und ihrem Beispiel zu erkennen.
Das bedeutet aber nicht, dass digitale Materialien für mich ungeeignet sind. Für das Wiederholen einzelner Wörter nutze ich gern eine digitale Liste. Ich kann sie auch unterwegs öffnen und muss kein zusätzliches Heft mitnehmen. Schwierig wird es, wenn ich beim Lesen ständig zwischen mehreren Anwendungen wechsle. Dann beschäftige ich mich manchmal mehr mit den Werkzeugen als mit dem Inhalt.
Eine Freundin von mir sieht das anders. Sie ordnet ihre Notizen digital und findet Informationen dadurch schneller wieder. Unsere unterschiedlichen Erfahrungen zeigen mir, dass ich nicht einfach eine Methode für alle empfehlen kann.
Für meine eigene Arbeit habe ich deshalb eine kleine Regel gewählt: Einen längeren neuen Text lese ich zuerst in Ruhe auf Papier. Später übertrage ich nur die wichtigsten Wörter und Fragen in meine digitale Sammlung. So bekommt jedes Werkzeug eine klare Aufgabe. Wenn sich mein Alltag ändert, kann ich diese Aufteilung wieder anpassen.

The writer prefers paper for first reading of longer texts because marginal notes help them see the reasoning. They still use digital word lists and acknowledge a friend’s different experience. The conclusion gives each tool a specific job and leaves room to change the arrangement later.

Try the questions before reading the English overview again. The overview is a meaning check, not the source of extra facts to add to the passage. When a detail is absent, distinguish “not stated” from “false”: those are different reading decisions. Do not infer a real timetable, service policy or general research finding from this fictional text.

Try it yourself

Write a short answer to each question and identify the sentence or phrase that supports it. Do not use a likely real-world answer if the passage says something different. The key explains why the answer follows from this particular text.

  1. What is the writer’s initial preference for a long new text?
  2. What do marginal notes help the writer identify?
  3. Does the writer reject digital tools?
  4. Why mention the friend’s different experience?
  5. What is the final arrangement?

Answers and explanations

Show answers after your attempt
  1. What is the writer’s initial preference for a long new text?

    Reading on paper. The preference is scoped to that activity, not every study task.

  2. What do marginal notes help the writer identify?

    The link between a claim and its example. The first paragraph gives a reason rather than a numerical research finding.

  3. Does the writer reject digital tools?

    No. They use a digital vocabulary list and collection.

  4. Why mention the friend’s different experience?

    To limit a universal recommendation. The writer recognises that another method can work for someone else.

  5. What is the final arrangement?

    Paper for the first long reading, selected words and questions stored digitally. The conclusion assigns each tool a job and allows later adaptation.

Use it in a new situation

Write a one-sentence summary, then find one passage that supports it and one that limits it. If your summary says “paper is always better,” compare it with the friend’s example and rewrite it. A good reading summary preserves the writer’s scope instead of turning a personal preference into a universal fact.

Finish by writing a short summary without adding facts from outside the passage. Reopen the source and remove any detail you cannot support. Then choose one unfamiliar expression and study its role in the sentence. This combines meaning, evidence and vocabulary work instead of turning reading into the translation of isolated words.

Questions

Should I translate the entire passage first?

Try identifying its purpose and requested details first. Use the glossary for a focused meaning check, then return to the relevant sentence.

Can I answer from general knowledge?

Base comprehension answers on this fictional passage. A plausible real-world fact does not supply evidence for something the text does not say.

Does a correct answer establish my CEFR level?

No. One short reading activity cannot certify a level or predict a full examination result. The level in the title describes the intended practice context.

What should I do after checking the key?

Locate the supporting phrase for each answer, explain any error and summarise the text in your own words. Revisit a specific unclear expression rather than rereading without a purpose.
