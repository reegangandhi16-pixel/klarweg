Which German level do you need for an Ausbildung?

Short answer: B1 is the legal standard for an Ausbildung visa, and many employers, especially in nursing and care, ask for more. The longer answer depends on three different gatekeepers, and it is worth understanding each before you choose a course or book an exam.

Based on the German Residence Act (AufenthG §2, §16a and §17) · Last checked 5 November 2026 · This is general information, not legal advice

In 30 seconds

  • For a residence permit for a qualified Ausbildung, German law asks for "sufficient" German, defined as B1.
  • There is an exception: proof is not required if your training provider has already checked your German for that training, or if you will first attend a preparatory German course in Germany.
  • To come to Germany to look for a training place (up to nine months), you also need B1, plus other conditions.
  • Employers and vocational schools set their own expectations. In nursing and care, B2 is often asked for.

What the law says

The German Residence Act defines language levels precisely. "Sufficient" German knowledge (ausreichende deutsche Sprachkenntnisse) means B1 of the Common European Framework of Reference (§2(11) AufenthG). Two rules use it for Ausbildung:

SituationLanguage ruleOther conditions
Residence permit for a qualified Ausbildung (§16a)Proof of B1 is required, unless the training provider has checked the German needed for that specific training, or the German is to be acquired through a preparatory German course.Approval of the Federal Employment Agency where required; a training contract or school place.
Residence permit to look for a training place (§17)B1 required.Under 35; secured living costs; a school-leaving certificate that gives access to university in Germany or in the country where it was obtained. Granted for up to nine months.

Two practical consequences. First, the exception in §16a is decided by your training provider's assessment or a planned course, not by you; if you cannot document either, plan on a B1 certificate. Second, the German mission that processes your visa decides which proof it accepts, so check its current list before you book an exam.

The three gatekeepers

An Ausbildung in Germany usually combines work in a company with classes at a vocational school (Berufsschule). That means three separate parties care about your German:

  1. The authorities (German mission, foreigners' office): the legal minimum above, normally B1.
  2. The employer or training company: whatever level they write into the offer. Many set B1; healthcare employers often want B2.
  3. The vocational school and the final exam: lessons, textbooks and the final examination are in German, with technical vocabulary. Nobody stamps a level on this gate, but it is where weak German shows.

The visa only needs the first gate. Finishing the Ausbildung needs the third. That is why aiming slightly above the minimum is usually the cheaper choice in the long run.

When B2 makes sense

Requirements differ by occupation, employer and federal state, so treat this as orientation, not a rule:

  • Nursing and care (Pflege): communication with patients is safety-critical, and B2 is often asked for. Some providers accept B1 with a plan to reach B2 during training. Full details: German for nurses in Germany.
  • Customer-facing roles (hospitality, retail, office work): B1 is common, but fluent speaking helps in interviews and at work.
  • Technical trades and IT: B1 is a common starting point; the vocational school's technical German is the real challenge.

If an offer names a level, that level is your target. If it does not, ask before you sign.

Which certificate?

Look for an established, standardised exam that tests all four skills (reading, listening, writing, speaking). Widely used options include the Goethe-Zertifikat B1, telc Deutsch B1 and ÖSD Zertifikat B1. Before booking, confirm two things in writing: which exams your employer accepts, and which the German mission accepts for your visa category. For Goethe exams in India, see fees and registration.

How long it takes from zero

The Goethe-Institut's rough guideline for completing B1 is about 350–650 teaching units of 45 minutes, counted from the beginning, which is roughly 260–490 hours of guided learning. At about ten hours a week, that is roughly six months to a year, before exam preparation. Your own pace depends on prior exposure, consistency and how much you speak.

Checklist before you apply

  • Ask the employer which German level and which certificates they accept.
  • Ask whether they assess your German themselves. This matters for the §16a exception.
  • Check the German mission's current requirements for your visa category.
  • Book your exam early, because exam dates and seats are limited (how booking works).
  • Keep scanned copies of every certificate and result sheet.

Questions

Is German compulsory for an Ausbildung?

In practice, yes. Even where the visa rule allows an exception, training and the vocational school are in German. The question is only whether you need a certificate before you arrive.

Is A2 enough?

A2 is below the legal B1 standard. It may only work if your training provider has assessed your German and accepts it, or if a preparatory German course in Germany is part of your plan. Treat it as a stepping stone, not a target (Goethe A2 exam guide).
