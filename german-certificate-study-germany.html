Which German certificate do you need to study in Germany?

For a degree taught in German, the rules are more precise than most advice suggests: German universities work from a national framework that names the accepted exams. This guide explains that list, why a Goethe C1 is not automatically enough, when you need no German at all, and how long it takes to get there.

Based on the national framework for German language exams for university study (RO-DT, HRK/KMK, 2020 version) · Last checked 5 November 2026 · Your university's own rules always take precedence

In 30 seconds

  • For full admission to a German-taught programme, the framework accepts DSH-2, TestDaF with at least TDN 4 in all four parts, the German part of the Studienkolleg exam (Feststellungsprüfung), or the DSD II.
  • Also exempt from further proof: Goethe-Zertifikat C2, ÖSD C2 and telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule.
  • The Goethe-Zertifikat C1 is not on that list. Some universities accept it under their own rules; many do not. Check before you book.
  • Programmes taught in English often need no German for admission.

The accepted certificates

German universities follow a framework agreed by the German Rectors' Conference (HRK) and the Standing Conference of education ministers (KMK), the Rahmenordnung über Deutsche Sprachprüfungen für das Studium (RO-DT). It lists the proofs that count for admission to all programmes:

telc Deutsch C1 HochschulePassedA C1 exam designed for university entry; exempts you from further proof.

School-leaving certificates equivalent to a German Abitur also exempt you, and some other foreign certificates are recognised by the KMK.

What about Goethe C1?

The general Goethe-Zertifikat C1 is a well-respected certificate, but it is not among the proofs the framework names. A university may still accept it under its own admission rules, and some do. Others insist on DSH, TestDaF or telc C1 Hochschule. Because these exams cost money and months of preparation, find your programme's language requirement on its official admission page before choosing an exam.

If you aim at Goethe exams, the Goethe-Zertifikat C2 is the one the framework names.

When universities ask for less, or more

The framework lets universities set different entry levels depending on the programme:

  • Lower: some programmes admit students with DSH-1 or TDN 3, usually on condition that they take German courses alongside their studies. Such an admission may not carry over if you change programme or university.
  • Higher: programmes with heavy language demands, such as German studies, can ask for more, for example DSH-3.

Universities publish these requirements with their application information. That page, not a general guide, is the final word.

Bachelor, Master, Studienkolleg, English programmes

  • Bachelor's in German: depending on your school certificate, you may first need a Studienkolleg, a preparatory year that ends with the Feststellungsprüfung. The DAAD's admission information tells you which route applies to your certificate. Studienkollegs set their own entrance tests and German requirements, often around B1.
  • Master's in German: the same language proofs as above, plus your degree's subject requirements.
  • Programmes in English: often no German is required for admission. German still matters for part-time jobs, internships, daily life and working in Germany after graduation; many international students learn to B1 or B2 alongside their studies.

How long it takes

The Goethe-Institut's rough guideline for completing C1 is about 800–1,000 teaching units of 45 minutes, counted from the beginning, which is roughly 600–750 hours of guided learning. At ten hours a week, that is well over a year, before exam preparation. For an autumn intake, start early, and plan your exam date backwards from the application deadline.

Checklist

  • Find the official admission page of each programme you are considering, and note the exact language proofs it accepts.
  • Check whether your school certificate gives direct access or requires a Studienkolleg.
  • Choose the exam that all your target programmes accept, not the one that is easiest to book.
  • Plan the exam date at least a few months before the application deadline, to leave room for a retake.
  • Keep certified copies of certificates; some application portals ask for them.
