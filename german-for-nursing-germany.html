How much German do you need to work as a nurse in Germany?

There are two routes into nursing in Germany, and they ask for different German. If you are not yet a nurse, you train through a three-year nursing Ausbildung. If you already hold a nursing qualification from India, you apply to have it recognised. This guide explains the language level for each route, based on the law and on the official recognition portal.

Based on the Nursing Professions Act (PflBG), the Residence Act (AufenthG §2, §16a, §16d), the official recognition portal and the Federal Employment Agency · Last checked 5 November 2026 · General information, not legal advice

In 30 seconds

  • Route 1, nursing Ausbildung (training as Pflegefachfrau/Pflegefachmann, three years full-time): the visa standard is B1, with exceptions; many schools and employers ask for B2.
  • Route 2, recognition of an Indian nursing qualification: the official portal says you need B2; some states require nursing-specific German or a nursing language exam.
  • You can sometimes enter Germany earlier, for recognition measures or a recognition partnership with an employer, with German at A2 as a rule, and reach B2 there.
  • Either way, the final licence requires the German you need to do the job safely.

Route 1: nursing Ausbildung

Since 2020, nursing training in Germany is generalist, leading to the title Pflegefachfrau or Pflegefachmann. Full-time, it lasts three years, with classroom teaching at a nursing school and practical training, and the practical part is the larger one (PflBG §6). Entry normally requires a school certificate equivalent to the German mittlerer Schulabschluss (PflBG §11); whether and how your Indian certificate counts is decided by the competent authority.

The language requirement

  • Visa: for a qualified Ausbildung, the Residence Act asks for "sufficient" German, defined as B1 (AufenthG §16a, §2). This is not required if the training provider has already checked your German for that training, or if you will first attend a preparatory German course. Details in German level for Ausbildung.
  • School and employer: nursing school is taught in German, with medical vocabulary from the first weeks, and patients must understand you. That is why many nursing schools and employers ask for B2, or for B1 with a plan to reach B2 early in training.

Practical advice: aim for B2 if you can. B1 opens the door, but B2 is what makes the first year manageable.

Route 2: recognition of your qualification

If you are already a nurse, you apply for recognition (Anerkennung) of your qualification and for permission to use the professional title. The authority compares your training with the German one (PflBG §40). If it finds substantial differences, you can close them with an adaptation course or a knowledge exam.

The language requirement

  • For the licence, the law asks for the German needed to practise the profession (PflBG §2 no. 4). The official recognition portal states this as B2.
  • Some federal states require the B2 to be nursing-specific, or ask for a nursing language exam (Fachsprachenprüfung Pflege). In some states you can submit the language proof later in the procedure.
  • To come to Germany for recognition measures (such as an adaptation course), or under a recognition partnership with a German employer, the Residence Act asks for German that fits the measure, as a rule at least A2 (AufenthG §16d, §2). You then continue to B2 in Germany.

The rules are set by the federal states and change. The official Anerkennung in Deutschland portal has a "Recognition Finder" that shows the authority and requirements for the state where you want to work.

What nursing German sounds like

Exam German and ward German overlap less than people expect. Nurses talk to patients, relatives, doctors and colleagues all day, always using the polite Sie with patients.

  • Haben Sie Schmerzen? Wo tut es Ihnen weh? Are you in pain? Where does it hurt?
  • Ich messe jetzt Ihren Blutdruck. I'm going to take your blood pressure now.
  • Bitte nehmen Sie diese Tablette nach dem Essen. Please take this tablet after your meal.
  • Haben Sie heute schon etwas getrunken? Have you had anything to drink today?
  • Ich sage dem Arzt Bescheid. I'll let the doctor know.

Beyond conversation, nurses write: care documentation (Pflegedokumentation) and shift handovers (Übergabe). Clear past tenses and precise time expressions matter here, which is exactly what B1 and B2 build.

How long it takes

The Federal Employment Agency's recruitment programme for hospitals plans a full-time German course in the home country of about 7–8 months to a B1 certificate, or about 10–12 months to B2, before the nurse moves to Germany. Learning part-time, alongside a job, takes considerably longer. The Goethe-Institut's rough guideline for completing B2 is about 600–800 teaching units of 45 minutes from the beginning, roughly 450–600 hours.

Checklist

  • Decide your route: Ausbildung (not yet a nurse) or recognition (already a nurse).
  • For recognition: use the Recognition Finder on the official portal for the state where you want to work, and note its language rule.
  • For Ausbildung: ask the nursing school and employer which level and certificate they require.
  • Book a standardised exam that tests all four skills, such as the Goethe-Zertifikat B1 or B2 (fees and booking in India).
  • Be careful with agencies that promise a job "without German". Every route ends with the German needed to work safely.
