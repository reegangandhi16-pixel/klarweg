Inviting Friends in German with Hindi

Use this guide to invite somebody, give a practical detail and respond politely. Work through original German examples, Hindi meaning checks and exercises with explanations.

Understand this topic

An invitation tells the other person what you propose and gives enough detail for a decision. An acceptance may still need a time or place question. A decline can include a brief reason without making an elaborate excuse. The friends in this exchange use du. Do not switch to Sie halfway through unless you are deliberately changing the relationship in a new exercise.

A conversation to understand and adapt

Original fictional dialogue

A: Hast du am Samstag Zeit?
B: Ja. Was hast du vor?
A: Ich möchte im Park picknicken. Möchtest du mitkommen?
B: Gern. Wann treffen wir uns?
A: Um zwei Uhr am Eingang.
B: Soll ich etwas mitbringen?
A: Vielleicht etwas Obst.
B: Gut, bis Samstag!

Read the meaning notes below before rehearsing with a partner.

Follow what each turn does
TurnMeaning
A: Hast du am Samstag Zeit?Do you have time on Saturday?
B: Ja. Was hast du vor?Yes. What are you planning?
A: Ich möchte im Park picknicken. Möchtest du mitkommen?I would like to have a picnic in the park. Would you like to come?
B: Gern. Wann treffen wir uns?Gladly. When shall we meet?
A: Um zwei Uhr am Eingang.At two by the entrance.
B: Soll ich etwas mitbringen?Shall I bring something?
A: Vielleicht etwas Obst.Perhaps some fruit.
B: Gut, bis Samstag!Good, see you Saturday!

Read one turn, hide its response and decide what information the speaker needs next. Then uncover the answer and compare its function with your own. A different answer can be appropriate if it is clear, relevant and grammatical. Do not treat this single model as the only permitted wording for the situation.

Try it yourself

Respond to the prompts without reading the dialogue. For an open response, aim to perform the requested action with information you understand. The key gives one possible wording, not an official scoring rule. Compare the response’s purpose as well as its grammar.

  1. What activity is proposed?
  2. Decline because you have no time.
  3. Ask when you will meet.

Answers and explanations

Show answers after your attempt
  1. What activity is proposed?

    A picnic in the park. Read the activity rather than only the day.

  2. Decline because you have no time.

    Leider habe ich keine Zeit. This is an informal situation, but the statement itself is neutral.

  3. Ask when you will meet.

    Wann treffen wir uns? Wann asks for time, not place.

Use it in a new situation

Change the picnic into an evening at home or a visit to a museum. Give a new place and time, then practise both accepting and declining. Add one useful follow-up about what to bring or how to meet. An invitation is complete when the other person can act on its details.

For a second round, exchange roles and remove the script. Give your partner one new detail to react to. Afterward, each person summarises the agreed information or outcome. Compare those summaries: a grammatically accurate turn is not enough if the speakers leave with different understandings of the practical arrangement.

Questions

Do I have to memorise the whole dialogue?

Learn what each turn does, then change a practical detail. Being able to respond to a new question is more useful than reciting an unchanged script.

Can I use a different response?

Yes, when it performs the required action clearly and correctly. The answer key gives sample wording and does not replace feedback on every possible response.

Should I use du or Sie?

Follow the relationship in the model and in your actual situation. Keep the chosen form consistent; a new relationship may require changes across the sentence.

Are these official examination questions?

No. The dialogue and tasks are original fictional practice for invite somebody, give a practical detail and respond politely. They do not predict an examination card.
