German language levels A1–C2: differences and next steps

Source-checked learning guide · Last checked 9 November 2026

German language levels run from A1 to C2. Use them to choose a learning target, but keep three things separate: what you can do in German, the course you have attended and the certificate an organisation requests. This comparison explains the level scale and helps you decide what to check before buying a course or booking an assessment.

German A1–C2 at a glance

The Council of Europe’s CEFR describes six proficiency levels, grouped into basic, independent and proficient use. The short descriptions below are orientation summaries, not a placement test or the full descriptors.

A practical orientation to the CEFR levels

A2Routine exchanges about familiar everyday needs.A2 course

A1 versus A2: build beyond a first lesson

A1 is the starting point for a new learner; A2 develops beyond that first foundation. For course selection, ask what the next class expects you to do independently, how much revision it includes and how the teacher checks the starting point. A course title alone cannot tell you whether you are ready for its activities.

Course labels such as A1.1 and A1.2 indicate subdivisions used by a provider. Check what the specific programme covers before comparing a partial volume or short course with a complete-level offering. Goethe’s FAQ explains its own subdivisions; another provider may package teaching differently.

B1 versus B2: check the depth of the task

Goethe’s B1 description centres on familiar topics and handling everyday situations; its B2 description includes more complex content and explaining viewpoints in detail. See the provider’s B1 information and B2 information for its own descriptions.

When comparing courses, request a sample task and ask how feedback is given. Being able to recognise an answer after reading a key is different from producing a clear response without prompts. Try a fresh reading, listening, writing and speaking activity, then identify which skill needs support rather than choosing a level from one remembered score.

For the existing preparation overviews, use the B1 guide and B2 guide. They address exam preparation; this page helps you compare learning targets. Neither course attendance nor reading a guide awards a certificate.

C1 and C2: assess the context, not a label

If your goal involves advanced study or work, get feedback on tasks resembling that context. Ask someone qualified to review how you structure an argument, understand demanding material and express a distinction accurately. Do not choose an advanced course because its label sounds more valuable than the one matching your current skills.

Look at the C1 roadmap or C2 roadmap as a course outline, then confirm starting requirements. For a particular application, the receiving organisation determines which evidence it accepts. A higher-sounding course label does not remove the need to check that requirement.

Choose a starting level with evidence

  1. Collect a recent sample. Use something you attempted without help rather than an old certificate alone.
  2. Check all relevant skills. Include spoken and written production, not only multiple-choice recognition.
  3. Ask for placement feedback. Show the teacher where you needed prompts and where you could continue independently.
  4. Read the course prerequisites. Confirm what is assumed and what revision is available.
  5. Review after starting. If most tasks are inaccessible, discuss the placement before adding more unrelated materials.

A period away from study can change what you can retrieve quickly. Equally, familiarity with one textbook does not mean every task will be equally comfortable. Tell the teacher about your study history and current goal so the recommendation reflects both.

Match your target to the organisation requesting it

For learners in India planning a move, different employers, institutions and routes can ask for different evidence. Check the original requirement, exact certificate name and any skill-specific conditions directly with the organisation. This comparison does not decide eligibility for a visa, job or course.

Questions

Is A1.2 a separate CEFR level?

It is a course subdivision, not an additional level in the six-level CEFR scale. Check the provider’s coverage and prerequisites.

Can I skip a level?

Use placement feedback and course requirements to decide. Prior learning may change your starting point; ambition alone is not evidence that prerequisite skills are in place.

Does completing B2 guarantee admission or employment?

No. Confirm the receiving organisation’s complete requirements. Language evidence is only one part of an application.
