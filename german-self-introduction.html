German Self Introduction with Hindi Meanings

Use this guide to introduce yourself and answer a follow-up instead of reciting one fixed speech. Work through original German examples, Hindi meaning checks and exercises with explanations.

Original Klarweg learning material · 9 November 2026

Understand this topic

Prepare information in small blocks: name, home, languages, work or study, and interests. Use the blocks that fit the situation. A classroom introduction may be informal, while an unfamiliar adult interaction may use Sie. Do not copy someone else’s occupation or age just because its sentence is easy. The fictional exchange starts politely and keeps each answer tied to the question.

A conversation to understand and adapt

Original fictional dialogue

A: Guten Tag. Wie heißen Sie?
B: Ich heiße Anika Rao.
A: Woher kommen Sie?
B: Ich komme aus Indien und wohne in Pune.
A: Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie?
B: Ich spreche Hindi und Englisch. Ich lerne Deutsch.
A: Was machen Sie gern in Ihrer Freizeit?
B: Ich lese gern und gehe oft spazieren.

Read the meaning notes below before rehearsing with a partner.

Follow what each turn does
TurnMeaning
A: Guten Tag. Wie heißen Sie?Good day. What is your name?
B: Ich heiße Anika Rao.My name is Anika Rao.
A: Woher kommen Sie?Where do you come from?
B: Ich komme aus Indien und wohne in Pune.I come from India and live in Pune.
A: Welche Sprachen sprechen Sie?Which languages do you speak?
B: Ich spreche Hindi und Englisch. Ich lerne Deutsch.I speak Hindi and English. I am learning German.
A: Was machen Sie gern in Ihrer Freizeit?What do you enjoy doing in your free time?
B: Ich lese gern und gehe oft spazieren.I enjoy reading and often go for a walk.

Read one turn, hide its response and decide what information the speaker needs next. Then uncover the answer and compare its function with your own. A different answer can be appropriate if it is clear, relevant and grammatical. Do not treat this single model as the only permitted wording for the situation.

Try it yourself

Respond to the prompts without reading the dialogue. For an open response, aim to perform the requested action with information you understand. The key gives one possible wording, not an official scoring rule. Compare the response’s purpose as well as its grammar.

  1. Answer the name question with the fictional name Ravi.
  2. Ask where someone lives, using Sie.
  3. Say that you enjoy reading.

Answers and explanations

Show answers after your attempt
  1. Answer the name question with the fictional name Ravi.

    Ich heiße Ravi. Give a name rather than restarting every detail.

  2. Ask where someone lives, using Sie.

    Wo wohnen Sie? Wo asks about location; woher asks about origin.

  3. Say that you enjoy reading.

    Ich lese gern. Gern expresses enjoyment in this pattern.

Use it in a new situation

Ask a partner to change the question order. Begin with hobbies, then languages and finally the name. Give only the requested information on the first attempt. On the next attempt, add one relevant detail. This variation checks flexible retrieval and helps you notice which question you understood rather than merely anticipating the next line.

For a second round, exchange roles and remove the script. Give your partner one new detail to react to. Afterward, each person summarises the agreed information or outcome. Compare those summaries: a grammatically accurate turn is not enough if the speakers leave with different understandings of the practical arrangement.

Questions

Do I have to memorise the whole dialogue?

Learn what each turn does, then change a practical detail. Being able to respond to a new question is more useful than reciting an unchanged script.

Can I use a different response?

Yes, when it performs the required action clearly and correctly. The answer key gives sample wording and does not replace feedback on every possible response.

Should I use du or Sie?

Follow the relationship in the model and in your actual situation. Keep the chosen form consistent; a new relationship may require changes across the sentence.

Are these official examination questions?

No. The dialogue and tasks are original fictional practice for introduce yourself and answer a follow-up instead of reciting one fixed speech. They do not predict an examination card.
