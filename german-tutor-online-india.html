How to Choose an Online German Tutor in India

Quick answer

Before paying a German tutor, check six things: levels and exam experience, Hindi or English support, how much you speak in the trial, how they correct you, a fixed IST slot, and the total fee per level. Six checks before you pay any German tutor

CheckWhat a good answer looks likeWarning sign
Level and exam experienceThe tutor names the CEFR levels they teach and, if you need an exam, which exam formats they prepare for.“All levels, every exam” without examples.
Teaching languageThey can explain grammar in Hindi or English when you are stuck, and switch to German as you progress.German only from the first day, if you are a beginner.
Speaking timeIn a trial, you speak for at least half of the time.The tutor talks and you listen.
CorrectionsOne or two clear corrections that you can repeat on your own after the lesson.Many corrections at once, or none at all.
IST scheduleA fixed weekly slot you can actually attend, and a written rule for rescheduling.“We will see” about timing.
Total feeA full course fee per level, what it includes, and whether the exam fee is separate.Only an hourly rate with no total, or a “certificate” that is not an exam.

Send the same brief to each tutor

Include your level, one task you want help with and two possible times in IST. For example: “I am at A2, I want to handle short phone calls, and I am free after 7 p.m. on Tuesday or Thursday. How do you correct mistakes, and what do I practise between lessons?” The same brief makes the replies easy to compare.

Test the tutor with a phone task in the trial

Ask the tutor to say a meeting time once. If you miss it, ask for repetition. Then repeat the day and time back to confirm.

Check one possible answer

Können Sie die Uhrzeit bitte wiederholen? Habe ich das richtig verstanden: Mittwoch um halb drei?

halb drei is 2:30. After the trial, note whether the tutor let you speak, gave a correction you understood and had a slot you can reliably attend.
