German writing practice: six prompts and a revision checklist

Original practice guide · Last checked 9 November 2026

German writing practice is easier to review when the reader, purpose and required details are clear. The original tasks below cover short everyday messages and a slightly longer explanation. Choose a task matching material you have studied, write without copying a model, then revise against a focused checklist. This is general practice, not an official exam paper or marking scheme.

Choose a task you can attempt independently

Start with something familiar enough that you can concentrate on expressing it. A short message with a real purpose is a more useful starting point than a long essay whose vocabulary you have not studied. If you need substantial help for every sentence, reduce the task or return to a more suitable lesson.

The suggested lengths here are optional practice limits. They are not Goethe, telc or CEFR requirements, and they do not establish a proficiency level. Use your teacher’s actual instructions when completing a course assignment or an official practice task.

Six original writing prompts

1. Introduce yourself to a learning group

Write a few sentences for a new classmate. Include your name, where you live, the languages you use and one reason you are learning German. Share only details you want to include. Check whether a reader can find those points without guessing.

2. Arrange a study meeting

Write a short message to a friend. Suggest a day, a time and a place or online meeting method. Ask whether the arrangement works. On revision, underline the details the other person needs to respond.

3. Change an appointment

Write to someone you already have an arrangement with. Explain that you need a change, give a brief reason and suggest an alternative. Decide who the recipient is before choosing the style of address. Keep the purpose clear without adding an unrelated story.

4. Describe a normal day

Write a short paragraph about several activities in a normal day. Put them in order and include details you can express with language you have already studied. Then check that the time expressions and the sequence do not contradict each other.

5. Ask about a course

Write a message asking for two pieces of information you genuinely need, such as the starting requirement and meeting schedule. Include enough context for the recipient to understand your question. Check that you have asked for information rather than simply listing topics.

6. Explain a study choice

Write a short explanation of why you currently prefer one study arrangement over another. Give a reason, acknowledge one limitation and finish with your decision. If that is too demanding, reduce it to a few clear sentences rather than borrowing an advanced paragraph you cannot explain.

For a brief session, choose one task and a small limit, such as a handful of sentences. For a longer session, add detail only when it helps the intended reader. More words do not automatically make the message clearer.

Plan the reader and message first

A simple drafting sequence
StepQuestion to answer
ReaderWho will read this, and what do they already know?
PurposeWhat should the reader understand or do afterwards?
ContentWhich details are required for that purpose?
OrderWhich point should come first so the message is easy to follow?
DraftCan you express it using structures you understand?

Use keywords for planning, not a full translation that you then convert word by word. Hindi or English notes can clarify the idea; return to the structures in your German lesson when drafting. Leave an uncertainty marked for feedback rather than hiding it inside a copied sentence.

The word-order guide and question-word guide can help when a sentence or question is the obstacle. Use the reference to solve that point, then return to the message as a whole.

Review in separate passes

Revision checklist for ordinary practice
PassWhat to checkA useful action
MessageAre the required details present and consistent?Underline each requested point in the draft.
ReaderIs the purpose clear and the style appropriate?Read the opening and request without the rest.
Sentence structureAre you using patterns you can explain?Check uncertain constructions against the lesson or a teacher.
Word formsWhich articles, endings or spellings are uncertain?Mark the specific word rather than rewriting everything.
Final readDoes the revised version still say what you intended?Compare it with the original task prompt.

Do not try to correct every kind of error at once. First check whether the message works; then examine the language choices that matter most for this attempt. If you focus only on spelling, you may miss an unanswered question or an inconsistent meeting time.

Keep the first draft beside the revision. Write one short note explaining a change. If you cannot explain a correction, ask for clarification before turning it into a rule in your notebook.

Ask for feedback you can use

Give the reviewer the task as well as the text. Ask whether the message is clear, which recurring issue deserves attention and what you should practise next. A correction without the original purpose may improve a sentence while missing the task.

If you use an automated tool, treat its suggestions as proposals rather than an authority on every language point. Ask a qualified teacher or fluent reviewer to resolve uncertainty. Do not submit an automatically rewritten text as evidence of independent ability if you cannot reproduce or explain its structures.

After reviewing, attempt a similar task with different details. That helps you check whether you learned a useful pattern rather than only memorising a corrected message. Record the points you can now handle and one remaining question.

Connect writing to listening and speaking

After a suitable listening activity, write a short summary and compare its main message with the source. After writing a meeting request, use a partner speaking activity to practise agreeing an arrangement aloud. These are different outputs, so do not assume success in one proves the other is equally comfortable.

Use the beginner self-study routine to reserve time for drafting and revision, not only for starting new tasks. The PDF resource directory and book guide can help you select supporting material without collecting unrelated worksheets.

Resources and questions

The Goethe-Institut free practice page links provider-hosted learner resources. Opened 9 November 2026. All six prompts and the review checklist here are original Klarweg editorial content, not reproduced official test questions.

Where are the model answers?

These open-ended prompts do not have one fixed answer. Use the content checklist and obtain feedback on your own attempt. This page does not present an unreviewed model paragraph as authoritative German.

Should I translate a complete Hindi draft?

You may use Hindi to organise ideas, but work with German structures you understand. If the intended message is beyond your current language, simplify the task or seek help rather than copying an unexplained translation.

Does this prepare me for an exam writing module?

It supports ordinary drafting and revision. For a particular exam’s tasks and criteria, use the exam preparation hub and official material rather than assuming these prompts match its format.
