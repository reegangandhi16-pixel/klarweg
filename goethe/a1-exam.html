Goethe A1 exam: pattern, syllabus and how to prepare

The Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 is the first official German certificate most learners in India take, for a spouse visa, a job application or simply to prove a solid start. Here is exactly what the exam contains, how it is marked, what you need to know, and a realistic eight-week plan.

Checked against the Goethe-Institut's exam regulations (version of 1 September 2025) and its A1 test description · Last checked 5 November 2026

In 30 seconds

  • Written exam, 65 minutes: Hören about 20 minutes, Lesen 25 minutes, Schreiben 20 minutes, with no breaks.
  • Speaking, 15 minutes in a group of up to four candidates, with no preparation time.
  • 100 points: up to 75 for the written exam and 25 for speaking. You pass with 60.
  • A1 is not modular: you sit the whole exam. (Modules you can book separately start at B1.)
  • Listed fee at Goethe-Institut / Max Mueller Bhavan in India: ₹9,400 (checked November 2026). Fees and booking →

Exam pattern and marks

Start Deutsch 1 has a written part and a spoken part. Both are compulsory. The written part normally comes first; if the two are held on different days, the missing part must be completed within 14 days.

Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 at a glance
PartTimeWhat you do
Hören≈ 20 min3 parts, 15 tasks: short everyday conversations, public announcements and phone messages.
Lesen25 min3 parts, 15 tasks: short notes and emails, small ads, signs and notices.
Schreiben20 minPart 1: fill five gaps in a form. Part 2: a short message of about 30 words.
Sprechen15 minIn a group: introduce yourself, ask and answer questions, make and respond to requests.

Your result is reported out of 100. The written exam is worth up to 75 points and speaking up to 25. The pass mark is 60 points. One consequence matters for planning: if you score below 35 in the written exam, you are not admitted to the speaking part, because 60 is no longer reachable. Strong reading and listening are your safety net.

A1 syllabus: what you need to know

A1 means you can handle the most predictable situations of daily life. The Goethe-Institut's test description says an A1 candidate can, for example:

  • introduce themselves, say what they do and where they live;
  • ask and answer simple questions about people, such as where someone lives;
  • ask someone for something and give something to someone;
  • fill in personal details on a form (name, nationality, age, date of birth);
  • write very short, simple messages such as a note or an email;
  • understand numbers, prices and times in short everyday texts.

Vocabulary

The official A1 word list has about 650 entries, grouped by everyday themes. At this level you are expected to use roughly half of them actively and recognise the rest. The list is printed at the end of the Goethe-Institut's A1 test description (PDF). Learn nouns with their article, never alone: der Tisch, die Tasche, das Buch.

Grammar

You do not need every rule, but these carry most A1 tasks:

  • Present tense, including sein and haben, and the verb in second position.
  • W-questions and yes/no questions: Wo wohnst du? · Hast du Zeit?
  • Articles and gender (der, die, das explained), ein and kein (nicht or kein?).
  • The accusative for objects (Akkusativ guide) and common dative phrases (Dativ guide).
  • Modal verbs können, möchten, müssen, separable verbs, polite requests and the Sie-imperative (modal verbs guide).
  • Numbers, prices, dates and clock times.

Hören: listening (about 20 minutes)

Three parts with 15 tasks in total. Parts 1 and 3 are played twice; part 2 is played only once, so read its questions before the audio starts.

  • Part 1: short conversations between two people, in the street, at home or at work.
  • Part 2: short public announcements, for example at a station or in a shop.
  • Part 3: short phone and voicemail messages.

Most answers are a time, a day, a price, a number or a place. Practise writing numbers as you hear them. Hindi helps here: German says einundzwanzig, literally "one-and-twenty", with the unit first, the same order as इक्कीस (एक + बीस). English speakers find this hard; you already think this way. Watch clock times, though: halb drei is 2:30 ("half to three"), not 3:30.

Lesen: reading (25 minutes)

Three parts with 15 tasks. You do not need to understand every word, only the detail each task asks about.

  • Part 1: two short notes or emails. Decide whether statements are richtig or falsch.
  • Part 2: five situations, each with two small ads. Choose the ad that fits. Usually one detail decides it: a day, a price, an age or an opening time.
  • Part 3: five short signs or notices, each with a true/false statement.

Mark every answer on the answer sheet: only what is on the answer sheet is marked.

Schreiben: writing (20 minutes)

Full guide: Goethe A1 Schreiben examples

Part 1 (5 points): a short text describes a situation; you use it to complete five missing details on a form, such as a name, a date or a number of people. Copy spellings exactly from the text.

Part 2 (10 points): write a short message of about 30 words that covers three given points, with a suitable greeting and closing. Each point you leave out costs marks, so cover all three, even briefly.

Example task

You cannot come to your German class tomorrow. Write to your teacher, Frau Weber: say that you cannot come, give a reason, and ask for the homework.

Model answer (about 30 words)

Liebe Frau Weber,
leider kann ich morgen nicht zum Kurs kommen. Ich bin krank und muss zum Arzt. Können Sie mir bitte die Hausaufgaben schicken?
Viele Grüße
Ravi

Dear Ms Weber, unfortunately I can't come to class tomorrow. I'm ill and have to go to the doctor. Could you please send me the homework? Best wishes, Ravi

Three details examiners notice: after the comma in Liebe Frau Weber, the next line starts with a small letter (leider); the modal verb sends the main verb to the end (kann … kommen); and every noun is capitalised (Kurs, Arzt, Hausaufgaben).

Sprechen: speaking (15 minutes, in a group)

Full guide: Goethe A1 Sprechen examples

Up to four candidates sit together with the examiners. There is no preparation time, so the phrases below need to come automatically.

Part 1: introduce yourself (3 points)

You get keywords such as Name? · Alter? · Land? · Wohnort? · Sprachen? · Beruf? · Hobby? and talk about yourself.

Example

Ich heiße Priya Sharma. Ich bin 24 Jahre alt und komme aus Indien. Ich wohne in Pune. Ich spreche Hindi, Marathi, Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch. Ich bin Krankenpflegerin von Beruf. Meine Hobbys sind Lesen und Kochen.

My name is Priya Sharma. I'm 24 and come from India. I live in Pune. I speak Hindi, Marathi, English and a little German. I'm a nurse. My hobbies are reading and cooking.

Part 2: ask and answer questions (6 points)

The group gets a theme, for example Essen und Trinken, and each candidate draws a word card. You make a question from your word and ask another candidate, who answers.

Frühstück → Was isst du zum Frühstück? — Ich esse meistens Brot und trinke Tee.

Part 3: make a request and respond (6 points)

Cards show an object or a situation. You ask someone to do something; they react.

Können Sie bitte das Fenster öffnen? — Ja, gern.

If you do not understand, ask: Wie bitte? Können Sie das bitte wiederholen? That is good communication, not a mistake.

Mistakes Hindi speakers make at A1

  1. Not Morgen ich nach Delhi fahre.But Morgen fahre ich nach Delhi. Hindi puts the verb at the end (मैं कल दिल्ली जाऊँगा). In a German main clause the verb is always the second element, even when the sentence starts with a time word.
  2. Not Ich habe 24 Jahre.But Ich bin 24 Jahre alt. Age uses sein plus alt.
  3. Not die Buch (because किताब is feminine)But das Buch Hindi gender does not carry over. Learn every noun with its article.
  4. Mixing du and Sie. Think of Sie as आप and du as तुम. With an examiner or a teacher, use Sie; with other candidates in the speaking test, du is normal.
  5. Writing nouns in small letters. In German every noun is capitalised: Ich trinke gern Tee.

Official sample papers

The Goethe-Institut publishes free practice sets (Übungssätze) for Start Deutsch 1, with answer keys and audio. Use them as rehearsals, not as material to memorise: the real exam uses new texts.

  • Start Deutsch 1, Übungssatz 02 (PDF)
  • Goethe-Institut India: A1 exam page, dates and practice material

Do the first set early, untimed, to see the format. Do the second under real time limits two weeks before the exam, and mark it honestly with the answer key.

An eight-week plan (after you finish A1 grammar)

The Goethe-Institut's rough guideline is about 80–200 teaching units of 45 minutes to complete A1 from zero. The plan below is for the final stretch, once you have worked through the A1 material.

WeeksFocusDaily routine
1–2Format and gapsOne untimed practice set. List every word and structure you missed. Revise numbers, times and dates.
3–4Reading and listeningOne reading part and one listening part a day. Read questions before the audio. Learn 15 words a day from the A1 list, with articles.
5–6Writing and speakingWrite one 30-word message a day covering three points; check verb position and capitals. Say your self-introduction aloud daily.
7Full rehearsalOne complete timed practice set. Practise speaking parts 2 and 3 with a partner.
8ConsolidateRework your mistake list only. Confirm exam time, centre address and the ID you must bring.

Questions

Is A1 enough for a spouse visa?

For joining a spouse in Germany, the usual requirement is basic German at A1, and Start Deutsch 1 is one of the accepted proofs. There are exceptions depending on your spouse's status, and the German mission that handles your visa decides what it accepts, so check its current requirements before you book.

Can I retake only the speaking part?

No. Start Deutsch 1 is a single exam with two compulsory parts, unlike B1–C2, where each module can be taken and retaken separately (how modules work).

Is Start Deutsch 1 the same as Fit in Deutsch 1?

Both are A1 exams from the Goethe-Institut. Start Deutsch 1 is the adult version; Fit in Deutsch 1 is designed for younger learners.

What comes after A1?

A2 builds past tense, more dative and longer connected sentences (Goethe A2 exam guide). If your goal is an Ausbildung, the target is B1: see the Goethe B1 exam guide and the German level needed for Ausbildung.
