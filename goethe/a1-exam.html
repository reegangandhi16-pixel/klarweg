Goethe A1 Exam: A Clear Guide to Start Deutsch 1

The Goethe-Zertifikat A1: Start Deutsch 1 checks whether you can understand and use simple German in familiar everyday situations. You do not need complicated grammar—you need clear, accurate basics and practice with the exam tasks.

HÖREN · LISTENING20 minShort everyday audio
LESEN · READING25 minNotes, notices, short texts
SCHREIBEN · WRITING20 minForm + short message
SPRECHEN · SPEAKING15 minOral group exam

The written sections take about 65 minutes in total. Speaking is a separate group exam; the official format allows up to four candidates and provides no preparation time. Always check the current instructions from your exam centre.

1. What each section tests

SectionWhat you doPractise this
HörenUnderstand short conversations, phone messages and announcements.Numbers, times, prices, names, places and key words.
LesenRead short notes, advertisements, signs and notices.Find the exact information; notice negation, dates and opening times.
SchreibenComplete a simple form and write a short personal message.Word order, greeting/closing, task points, spelling and basic sentence patterns.
SprechenIntroduce yourself, ask and answer everyday questions, make a request.Speak audibly, use complete short phrases and respond—not just memorise a speech.

2. Speaking: prepare these building blocks

Teil 1 — Vorstellung: practise a short introduction with your name, spelling, country, place of residence, languages, work/studies and interests.

Example: Ich heiße ___. Ich komme aus Indien und wohne in ___. Ich spreche Hindi, Englisch und ein bisschen Deutsch. Ich bin ___ von Beruf.
Meaning: My name is ___. I come from India and live in ___. I speak Hindi, English and a little German. I work as a ___.

Teil 2 — Fragen stellen und beantworten: practise asking for information using familiar question words: Wo? Wann? Wie viel? Wie spät? Was? For example: Wann fährt der Bus? — When does the bus leave?

Teil 3 — Bitten: practise simple requests and polite responses: Kannst du mir bitte einen Stift geben? — Can you please give me a pen? / Ja, gern. — Yes, gladly.

3. Writing: a reliable checklist

  • Read the task and identify every point you must include.
  • Use a suitable greeting: Liebe Maria / Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren.
  • Write short, correct sentences. A simple correct sentence is better than a risky long one.
  • Check verb position: Ich komme am Montag. (I am coming on Monday.)
  • Check nouns are capitalised, and include a closing when appropriate.

4. Common mistakes Hindi speakers can avoid

  • Translating word-for-word: learn German sentence patterns, not Hindi word order.
  • Forgetting the verb: in a basic statement, the conjugated verb is usually in position 2: Heute lerne ich Deutsch. (Today I am learning German.)
  • Skipping articles: learn nouns with their article and plural, e.g. der Termin — die Termine.
  • Speaking too fast: understandable pronunciation and a complete response matter more than speed.
  • Ignoring task points: in writing and speaking, answer what the prompt actually asks.

5. A practical 14-day preparation plan

DaysFocus
1–3Take a sample test diagnostically; revise introductions, numbers, time and everyday vocabulary.
4–6Practise listening and reading in timed sets; review every wrong answer.
7–9Write forms and short messages; get feedback on grammar and task completion.
10–12Practise speaking prompts with a partner; record yourself and improve clarity.
13Complete a full mock exam under timed conditions.
14Review recurring errors, prepare exam-day logistics and sleep well.

This is a sample schedule, not a guarantee of readiness. If your basics are not stable yet, spend more time learning before doing repeated mocks.
