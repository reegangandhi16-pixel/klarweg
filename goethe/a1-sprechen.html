Goethe A1 Sprechen: Teil 1, 2 and 3 examples

Goethe A1 Sprechen has three parts: introducing yourself, asking and answering questions, and making and responding to requests. This guide explains the task differences with original examples and Hindi meaning checks. The examples support practice; they are not official questions or a pass prediction.

Checked against official adult practice material and exam regulations · Last checked 9 November 2026

Quick answer

  • Three parts: introduce yourself, exchange information, then make and respond to requests.
  • The standard group speaking test lasts 15 minutes, with no preparation time. Official administration rules →
  • Study the original examples as flexible patterns, not a speech to memorise.

The three speaking parts at a glance

Goethe A1 adult speaking format
PartCommunicative jobPrepare this skill
1Introduce yourself and respond to short personal-information prompts.Give true details and follow the examiner’s question.
2Ask for information and answer another candidate.Build a question that fits the keyword and theme.
3Make a request and respond to one.Say what you want the other person to do.

The official adult model set describes these task types. The examples below are new Klarweg practice, not copied cards or model-test answers. Read the instructions on your actual paper; this guide does not predict which card you will receive.

There is a useful difference between knowing a phrase and using it for the task. A long description of your family will not replace a question about a particular object. Before speaking, identify the action required: introduce, ask, answer or request. That small decision keeps a familiar phrase from becoming an irrelevant answer.

Teil 1: introduce yourself clearly

Prepare the facts about yourself, not one rigid paragraph. You should be able to give a shorter version when asked for one detail and continue when invited to say more. Start with information you know how to express accurately. The following fictional introduction gives a compact model at beginner level.

Original Klarweg introduction · not an official exam item

Ich heiße Asha Mehta. Ich bin 26 Jahre alt. Ich komme aus Indien und wohne in Jaipur. Ich spreche Hindi und Englisch. Ich lerne Deutsch. Ich bin Studentin. Meine Hobbys sind Musik und Schwimmen.

My name is Asha Mehta. I am 26. I come from India and live in Jaipur. I speak Hindi and English. I am learning German. I am a student. My hobbies are music and swimming.

Replace the details with your own. Do not adopt a profession or hobby simply because it appears in this example. Read the model once, close it, and use a few English or Hindi keywords as prompts. On the next attempt, change the order in which someone asks for the information.

The model set also shows spelling and number follow-ups in the introduction stage. Practise hearing a request and responding to that request rather than restarting your whole introduction. For example, Bitte buchstabieren Sie Ihren Familiennamen. asks you to spell your surname. Use the alphabet guide and number guide to revisit the underlying language.

For names and numbers, clarity matters more than speed. In rehearsal, ask a partner to write what they heard, then compare it with the intended information. If you repeatedly lose one letter or digit, isolate that small part before returning to the complete exchange. This is a learning check, not a prediction of marks.

Teil 2: turn a keyword into a real question

In this part, a keyword belongs to a theme. Use both to decide what to ask. A rehearsed question may be grammatical and still fail to use the intended information. Read the full prompt first, then choose a question you can understand yourself.

Original Klarweg question exchange · not an official exam item

A: Wann lernen Sie Deutsch?
B: Ich lerne am Abend.
A: Lernen Sie allein?
B: Nein, ich lerne mit einer Freundin.

A: When do you study German? B: In the evening. A: Do you study alone? B: No, with a friend.

Original Klarweg second question exchange · not an official exam item

A: Wo kaufen Sie Obst?
B: Ich kaufe Obst auf dem Markt.
A: Kaufen Sie auch Brot?
B: Ja, im Supermarkt.

A: Where do you buy fruit? B: At the market. A: Do you also buy bread? B: Yes, at the supermarket.

These short exchanges illustrate question and answer practice; they are not a claim that every official card requires a follow-up question. The second turn in each example simply gives you an extra way to rehearse listening and responding. In the examination, follow the actual task and moderator.

For preparation, ask a partner to choose an ordinary topic you have studied. Make one information question, listen to the answer and summarise its meaning. Then change roles. If a question fails, use the question-word guide to understand the structure rather than memorising a correction without knowing what it asks.

Answer the question actually asked. If someone asks when you study, a list of the languages you speak does not supply the time. Add a short detail only when it helps. You do not need to turn every answer into a lengthy speech.

Teil 3: make a request and respond

A request asks someone to do something. It is different from asking for a fact about that person’s routine. Practise matching the action to the object or situation, then practise the response as a separate turn.

Original Klarweg request exchange · not an official exam item

A: Geben Sie mir bitte den Stift.
B: Ja, gern. Hier bitte.
A: Vielen Dank.

A: Please give me the pen. B: Gladly. Here you are. A: Thank you.

Original Klarweg second request exchange · not an official exam item

A: Können Sie bitte die Tür schließen?
B: Ja, natürlich.
A: Danke.

A: Could you close the door, please? B: Yes, of course. A: Thanks.

Use the action that makes sense. A pen can be passed to somebody; a door can be closed. In rehearsal, name the action in English or Hindi before saying the German request. This checks whether you are choosing a useful expression rather than attaching the same memorised sentence to every picture.

The response also belongs to the task. Listen to what the other person wants, then react. If you cannot follow the request, practise asking for repetition, for example Wie bitte?. Keep that recovery phrase available, but do not use it as a substitute for listening to the repeated message.

Hindi-first meaning checks

Geben Sie mir bitte den Stift. means कृपया मुझे कलम दीजिए। (kripaya mujhe kalam dijiye). Wann lernen Sie Deutsch? asks आप जर्मन कब सीखते हैं? (aap German kab seekhte hain). Notice the different jobs: one asks for an action; the other asks for information.

Use the Hindi meaning to confirm what the phrase does, then practise without reading the translation. Roman transliteration is supplied only as a reading aid for the Hindi gloss, not as a guide to German pronunciation. The German example and actual lesson audio remain the sound reference.

A rehearsal routine before official practice

Work in three small rounds: personal information, topic questions and requests. After each round, choose one difficulty to investigate. Was the question unclear, the answer unrelated, or a word hard to retrieve? Naming the problem is more useful than repeating the whole performance with the same uncertainty.

Then use a fresh official practice activity with a partner. Keep the official set separate from the examples here, and ask a qualified reviewer for feedback on comprehensibility and task completion. Questions

What happens in Teil 1, 2 and 3?

You introduce yourself, exchange information and make/respond to requests. The format table and linked official model set give the distinction.

Should I memorise an introduction?

Know your own information and useful sentence patterns, then practise responding in a different order. A fixed speech can make a simple follow-up harder to handle.

Should I use du or Sie?

The examples here use polite Sie. Follow the form used in the actual interaction and avoid switching forms accidentally within one exchange.

What if I do not understand a question?

Practise a short request for repetition, listen again and respond to the repeated question. Do not restart a memorised introduction instead.
