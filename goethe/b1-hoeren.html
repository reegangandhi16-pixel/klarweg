Goethe B1 Hören: listening strategy and practice

Goethe B1 Hören rewards prepared listening. Understand the format and permitted repetitions, then practise following meaning, detail and speaker changes.

Checked against Goethe-Institut regulations and official adult practice material · Last checked 8 November 2026

  • Pass: 60/100 for this module. Modules can be taken separately or together; a high score elsewhere does not replace this pass.

The parts at a glance

Goethe B1 Hören tests meaning under a fixed recording schedule. Listening well includes reading the question accurately before the voice starts. The adult practice format below separates the number of items from the number of recordings: one short recording can have more than one question.

B1 listening format
TeilRecordingTaskItemsHear it
1Five short textsTrue/false plus three-option choice10Twice
2A talk or announcementThree-option choice5Once
3Conversation between two peopleTrue/false7Once
4Presenter and guestsMatch statements to speakers8Twice

The module takes about 40 minutes. Follow the recorded instructions and pauses; you cannot redistribute minutes freely as you can in reading. The official paper practice material includes five minutes after listening to transfer answers. If you take a digital version, follow that version’s on-screen instructions rather than assuming paper transfer procedures apply.

Before, during and after listening

Teil 1: Short everyday messages

Before listening, separate the topic statement from the detail question. Predict the kind of information needed: a change, a request, a place or a time. During the first hearing, establish the situation and choose provisionally. During the second, check the decisive detail rather than mentally replaying the whole text. After the text, commit your answer and prepare for the next item. A familiar place name does not settle what the speaker wants you to do.

For this short exercise, read the questions first. Ask a partner to read the transcript twice while you keep the text covered. Then answer before looking at the key. Reading it silently is useful for analysing the language, but it does not measure listening comprehension.

Transcript for practice, read it aloud or have someone read it

Hallo Neha, hier ist Martin. Unser Treffen morgen bleibt um zehn Uhr. Wir treffen uns aber nicht im Café, sondern direkt vor der Bibliothek. Bring bitte dein Kursbuch mit. Einen Stift habe ich für dich dabei.

  1. Das Treffen beginnt später als geplant. Richtig oder falsch?
  2. Was soll Neha mitbringen? a Einen Stift. b Ihr Kursbuch. c Einen Kaffee.
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: Falsch. The time stays the same; only the meeting place changes.
  2. 2: b. Martin provides the pen, while Neha must bring her coursebook.

Review the correction or contrast in the transcript. Underline the words that replace the original plan, then say the final arrangement in English. If you chose the first detail you heard, practise holding an answer open until the message is finished. Do not replay a once-only task during a scored home attempt.

Teil 2: One longer information text

Before the recording, read all questions and underline a different key detail in each. Build a simple route through the information: arrival, activity, break, departure. During listening, stay with the current question and keep your notes short. If one answer is missed, mark uncertainty and continue; the recording will not wait. Afterwards, choose using the evidence you retained. Avoid changing an answer merely because the following question sounds similar.

For this short exercise, read the questions first. Ask a partner to read the transcript once while you keep the text covered. Then answer before looking at the key. Reading it silently is useful for analysing the language, but it does not measure listening comprehension.

Transcript for practice, read it aloud or have someone read it

Willkommen zum Fotoworkshop. Wir beginnen heute im Seminarraum im ersten Stock. Nach einer kurzen Einführung gehen wir in den Garten. Die Mittagspause beginnt um zwölf Uhr. Bitte lassen Sie Ihre Taschen im Schrank; Ihre Kamera brauchen Sie auch draußen.

  1. Wo beginnt der Workshop? a Im Garten. b Im Seminarraum. c In der Kantine.
  2. Was brauchen die Teilnehmenden draußen? a Ihre Kamera. b Ihre Tasche. c Einen Schrank.
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: b. The introduction is indoors; the garden comes later.
  2. 2: a. The camera is needed outside, while bags stay in the cupboard.

When checking your answer, separate the interviewer’s wording or the talk’s introduction from the information that actually answers the question. Retell the answer in one short sentence. If you understood it only after reading, record the phrase that your ear failed to recognise and revisit its sound in an official recording.

Teil 3: An everyday conversation

Before listening, identify names and the action in every statement. During the conversation, track who did, wanted or suggested each thing. Keep intentions separate from completed events. A speaker may describe a rejected plan before the final decision. Pay attention when the other person corrects a detail. After listening, do not infer an event just because it would normally happen in that situation. Each judgement must concern the statement as printed.

For this short exercise, read the questions first. Ask a partner to read the transcript once while you keep the text covered. Then answer before looking at the key. Reading it silently is useful for analysing the language, but it does not measure listening comprehension.

Transcript for practice, read it aloud or have someone read it

Anika: Hast du gestern die Wohnung besichtigt?
Lukas: Nein, die Vermieterin war krank. Wir haben einen neuen Termin für Freitag vereinbart.
Anika: Soll ich mitkommen?
Lukas: Gern. Diesmal fahren wir mit der Bahn. Den Bus nehmen wir nicht, weil die Straße gesperrt ist.

  1. Lukas hat die Wohnung gestern gesehen. Richtig oder falsch?
  2. Anika und Lukas wollen mit der Bahn fahren. Richtig oder falsch?
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: Falsch. The viewing was postponed because the landlady was ill.
  2. 2: Richtig. They choose the train and explicitly reject the bus.

After the key, label every statement by speaker. Notice when somebody quotes, summarises or responds to another person. In a longer recording, the same topic can pass between voices without the viewpoint passing with it. Practise tracking the source of the claim rather than relying on the last voice you heard.

Teil 4: A discussion with several speakers

Before listening, link each answer label to a person and role. During the first hearing, establish who holds which view, using the names and voice changes as anchors. During the second, confirm the statement rather than selecting the person who merely repeated a keyword. A presenter may ask about an opinion without sharing it. After the recording, check that you marked the correct speaker label for each question, not just the right idea.

For this short exercise, read the questions first. Ask a partner to read the transcript twice while you keep the text covered. Then answer before looking at the key. Reading it silently is useful for analysing the language, but it does not measure listening comprehension.

Transcript for practice, read it aloud or have someone read it

Moderator: Heute sprechen wir über gemeinsames Lernen. Frau Keller, was ist Ihnen wichtig?
Keller: Ich brauche Ruhe. In einer Gruppe verliere ich oft die Konzentration.
Yilmaz: Mir hilft es, wenn andere Fragen stellen. Dann merke ich, was ich noch nicht verstanden habe.
Moderator: Für Sie ist der Austausch also besonders hilfreich.

  1. Wer lernt lieber in Ruhe? a Moderator. b Keller. c Yilmaz.
  2. Wem helfen die Fragen anderer? a Moderator. b Keller. c Yilmaz.
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: b. Keller states that group work disrupts her concentration.
  2. 2: c. Yilmaz values other people’s questions; the presenter only summarises that view.

For a second learning pass, explain what each wrong option changes: person, timing, cause, scope or certainty. Then listen again with the transcript closed, using the permitted number of hearings. Repeated exposure helps language learning, but a remembered answer should never be counted as a fresh exam-readiness score.

Reading time and answer transfer

A practical listening routine
StageActionAvoid
Before the recordingRead the task and identify the distinction between options.Translating every word into Hindi.
First hearingFollow the meaning; note a provisional answer.Writing a transcript and missing the next item.
Second hearing, where providedCheck the exact reason, speaker or correction.Expecting a repeat in every part.
Paper transfer periodMatch each answer to its printed number.Shifting a whole block of answers by one row.

Use reading time for prediction, not for choosing an answer in advance. If all three options name a time, prepare to hear a correction or a change. If they name reasons, listen for cause rather than a repeated noun. Between recordings, release the previous question. You can mark uncertainty without letting it consume the next task. During paper transfer, check the first and last numbers of each block, then review blanks.

Training your ear from India

You can build a useful listening routine without living in Germany. Choose a short, level-appropriate recording from Deutsche Welle’s free German learning material or the Goethe-Institut’s official exam practice linked below. Start with a clip short enough to review properly. Listen once without a transcript and write its main point in one sentence. A vague topic label is not enough: say what changed, what was recommended or what the speaker believes.

Next, replay at a slower speed if the official player offers it, or work sentence by sentence. Use a transcript to locate where word boundaries disappeared for you. Close the transcript and listen at normal speed again. Slower listening is a training aid; your final exam rehearsal should use the original recording speed and official number of repetitions. Do not confuse understanding a familiar clip after several plays with understanding a new clip on the first hearing.

Try a brief dictation: select one sentence, write what you hear, then check only that sentence. Was the difficulty an unknown word, an unstressed ending or a familiar phrase pronounced as one unit? For shadowing, listen first and then repeat a short phrase with the speaker’s rhythm. Keep the phrase meaningful rather than racing to imitate every sound. Record your own version if helpful; the point is hearing the structure, not copying a particular accent.

For wider exposure, use free items on the ARD Mediathek or ZDF news site. Availability can vary by item and location. Choose a short report on a familiar subject, note who says what, and check any available subtitles afterwards. News language may be demanding at B1; return to learning material when too much vocabulary is unfamiliar. These broadcasts supplement, rather than replace, official exam preparation.

A manageable session might be twenty minutes: a first listen, a focused transcript check, a short dictation and a normal-speed replay. On another day, use a fresh clip with no replay until you have answered. Keep two separate records: learning sessions where pausing is allowed, and timed attempts where it is not. If you miss a question, note the cause before adding another hour of undirected listening.

Sound and number checks for Hindi speakers

Sound categories do not line up exactly across Hindi, English and German. Check ü/u and ö/o in real recordings rather than replacing them with a single familiar vowel. Round your lips for the umlaut vowels while listening to the tongue position demonstrated by a reliable teacher. Recognition comes before speed: a short contrast heard accurately is more useful than a long clip you can only partly follow.

Unstressed endings can be quiet. In mit einem guten Freund, the endings help identify the phrase even if they are not pronounced as separate syllables. Practise noticing final -en/-em in context. The spelling ch does not always represent one identical sound: compare ich and Buch using official recordings. A Devanagari approximation cannot represent every German sound precisely.

German numbers put the unit before the ten: einundzwanzig is 21. Hindi also has its own number forms, so do not rely on English word order as an intermediate step. Write digits directly when practising times and appointments. पहले पूरा अंक सुनें। reminds you to wait for the complete number before committing. Read the German numbers guide and German time expressions guide, then check recognition with fresh audio.

Meaning can hinge on a small word. Der Termin ist nicht am Dienstag, sondern am Donnerstag. replaces Tuesday with Thursday. Die Sitzung beginnt erst um neun. limits the start time. In your notebook, keep the complete phrase around the correction instead of listing only the two days or the numeral. Train these details in ordinary speech as well as in exam tasks.

Questions

Can I pause or request a replay?

In a full practice attempt, follow the official recording without adding pauses or repetitions. The table shows which parts provide a second hearing. Check the provider’s instructions for your booked format.

Should I translate while listening?

Aim to follow the German message directly. A quick English or Hindi note can help, but translating entire sentences often makes you miss the next piece of information.

Is reading a transcript enough?

No. It helps diagnose language problems but removes the listening task. Use it after an attempt, then return to audio with the text hidden.
