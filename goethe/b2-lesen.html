Goethe B2 Lesen: format and reading strategy

Goethe B2 Lesen rewards reading for evidence. Learn a method for each part with original mini-texts and answer explanations, then apply it to official practice.

The parts at a glance

Goethe B2 Lesen rewards accurate decisions about meaning. You do not need to translate every word. You do need to notice who acts, what is asserted and which conditions apply. The table describes the adult practice format; always read the instructions on your own paper.

A method for each part

Teil 1: Matching viewpoints across four texts

Create a compact mental profile for each writer: position, reason, limitation and change over time. Read each statement for its logical meaning before scanning for evidence. Several people may discuss the same subject while valuing it differently. Check whether the statement describes a preference, a necessity or a past habit. Return to the complete paragraph before assigning a person; one attractive keyword does not establish a match.

Original mini-text · strategy practice

A: Seit ich im Schichtdienst arbeite, nutze ich aufgezeichnete Vorlesungen. Auf den Austausch mit anderen möchte ich allerdings nicht verzichten.
B: Ich besuche lieber feste Seminare, obwohl die Anfahrt lange dauert. Ohne verbindliche Termine schiebe ich das Lernen häufig auf.
C: Für mich zählt vor allem, dass ich Rückfragen stellen kann. Ob der Kurs online stattfindet, ist zweitrangig.
D: Früher lernte ich ausschließlich allein. Inzwischen hilft mir eine kleine Gruppe, meine Fortschritte realistischer einzuschätzen.

  1. Wer braucht feste Termine, um regelmäßig zu lernen?
  2. Wer hat seine Lernweise verändert?
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: B. B links fixed appointments to avoiding procrastination.
  2. 2: D. D explicitly contrasts solitary learning in the past with group learning now.

After checking the key, find the exact words that made the wrong answer tempting. Name the difference in English before rereading the German. This turns a small exercise into a reusable checking habit. A correct guess is still worth reviewing if you cannot explain the evidence.

Teil 2: Reconstructing a text

Read the complete article once without filling anything in. At each gap, identify the relation needed: explanation, contrast, example or consequence. A candidate sentence must connect backwards and forwards. Check pronouns, demonstratives, tense and topic progression. Read the three-sentence sequence aloud in your head. Reject an option that fits grammatically but introduces a person or object never established. Follow the official instructions about unused sentences.

Original mini-text · strategy practice

Unsere Firma hat einen ruhigen Raum eingerichtet. [1] Deshalb bleiben Gespräche und Telefonate draußen. Anfangs wurde der Raum kaum genutzt. [2] Seitdem ist er oft schon morgens belegt.
A: Erst als die Regeln im Team besprochen wurden, stieg das Interesse.
B: Dort sollen Beschäftigte ungestört lesen und schreiben können.
C: Im kommenden Jahr wird das Gebäude verkauft.

  1. Welcher Satz passt in Lücke 1: A, B oder C?
  2. Welcher Satz passt in Lücke 2: A, B oder C?
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: B. The room’s purpose explains why conversations must stay outside.
  2. 2: A. The discussion supplies the turning point referred to by the final sentence.

For a second attempt, cover the options and answer the question in your own words. Then compare that answer with each option. This prevents familiar vocabulary from making the decision for you. Keep the original meaning, even when an answer uses a different expression.

Teil 3: Reading an argument precisely

Find the author’s claim and distinguish it from a quoted opinion. Questions may test reasons, consequences or qualifications rather than isolated details. Summarise the relevant paragraph in a short phrase before inspecting the options. Watch for an option that makes a limited claim universal or turns correlation into a cause. The right answer may use a synonym rather than any of the article’s most memorable words.

Original mini-text · strategy practice

Ein Betrieb testete eine kürzere Besprechungszeit. Zwar endeten die Sitzungen nun pünktlich, doch wichtige Fragen wurden anschließend oft in zusätzlichen Gesprächen geklärt. Die Projektleiterin hält den Versuch daher nur dann für sinnvoll, wenn Entscheidungen vorher besser vorbereitet werden. Eine reine Kürzung der Termine reiche nicht aus.

  1. Was schränkt den Erfolg ein? a Zusätzliche Gespräche sind nötig. b Niemand erscheint pünktlich. c Es gibt keine Projektleitung.
  2. Unter welcher Bedingung unterstützt die Leiterin den Versuch? a Wenn niemand Fragen stellt. b Wenn Entscheidungen besser vorbereitet werden. c Wenn alle Termine entfallen.
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: a. The work spills into extra conversations; punctual endings alone do not settle it.
  2. 2: b. Support is conditional on better preparation, not on removing discussion.

Make a two-column note after practice: requirements on the left, supporting evidence on the right. If one requirement has no evidence, keep looking rather than assuming it. This is especially useful when several texts concern the same activity but differ in availability or conditions.

Teil 4: Matching headings to opinions

Look for the central message of each opinion, not its opening example. Give it a short private heading before comparing the printed headings. Separate the writer’s judgement from background information. A heading that describes the topic may still miss the stance. Check the whole match set for competing candidates and observe the instructions about the example, unused text and reuse. Resolve ambiguity with the most specific evidence.

Original mini-text · strategy practice

A: Wer ständig erreichbar sein muss, kann sich kaum erholen. Nach Feierabend sollten Nachrichten warten dürfen, auch wenn die Technik eine sofortige Antwort ermöglicht.
B: Flexible Arbeitsorte funktionieren nur, wenn Aufgaben und Zuständigkeiten klar sind. Entscheidend ist deshalb weniger der Ort als die Absprache im Team.
C: Der kurze Weg ins Büro ist angenehm. Trotzdem arbeite ich lieber dort, weil persönliche Gespräche vieles erleichtern.

  1. Welche Äußerung passt zu „Klare Grenzen schützen die Freizeit“?
  2. Welche Äußerung passt zu „Organisation ist wichtiger als der Arbeitsort“?
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: A. A argues for boundaries on availability outside working hours.
  2. 2: B. B explicitly prioritises team arrangements over location.

Separate topic agreement from agreement with the proposal. Someone can approve of the goal yet reject the method. In your error log, quote the short phrase that determines the final position, then write what changed your first interpretation.

Teil 5: Locating a rule in a formal text

Start from the information need in the heading. Scan paragraph openings and repeated legal nouns to find the likely section, then read its limits carefully. Distinguish eligibility, procedure and consequences. A section that mentions documents is not necessarily about admission; it may describe later checks. Identify the operative verb and who it applies to. Formal nominal phrases become easier when you mentally turn them into simple clauses.

Original mini-text · strategy practice

§ 1: Die Teilnahme setzt eine vorherige Anmeldung voraus. Diese muss spätestens eine Woche vor Kursbeginn eingehen.
§ 2: Wer an zwei Terminen fehlt, erhält zusätzliche Aufgaben. Eine Bescheinigung wird erst nach deren Abgabe ausgestellt.
§ 3: Bei technischen Problemen informiert die Kursleitung alle Teilnehmenden per E-Mail über einen Ersatztermin.

  1. Wo finden Sie die Anmeldefrist?
  2. Wo wird erklärt, was nach Fehlzeiten erforderlich ist?
Answer key and explanation
  1. 1: § 1. The first section sets the registration deadline.
  2. 2: § 2. The second section links missed sessions to additional work before a certificate is issued.

For revision, change one condition in the question and decide whether the answer would change. This is a learning exercise, not a new exam item. It checks whether you understood the rule instead of simply remembering the answer letter.

Reading habits Hindi speakers can check

These are possible transfer difficulties, not assumptions about every Hindi speaker. Hindi often places the verb late; German main clauses usually place the finite verb second. Read the subject and finite verb first, then find any final infinitive or participle. Der Kurs findet trotz des Regens statt. means the course takes place despite the rain; the final separable element completes the action.

Track negation and quantity before deciding. Nicht alle Teilnehmer kommen aus Berlin. means not all participants come from Berlin, not that none do. सभी नहीं is a useful reminder of that limited scope. With kein, check the noun it negates; with nicht, identify the phrase being denied.

Connectors signal the direction of an argument. Es regnet, trotzdem gehen wir spazieren. expresses a contrast. Es regnet, deshalb bleiben wir zu Hause. gives a consequence. The Hindi contrast फिर भी and result इसलिए can help you check the relationship without translating the whole paragraph.

Do not let an English-looking word decide an answer. aktuell means current; eventuell means possibly. Read the surrounding sentence before attaching an English meaning. When an option contains a stronger claim such as “always”, “only” or “everyone”, return to the original limitation. A shared topic is not proof that two claims are equivalent.

Build a vocabulary notebook from errors rather than copying a whole article. Save the noun with its article and plural, the verb with its preposition, or a connector with a complete clause. Add a short English explanation of the relationship. Revisit a few entries the next day in a fresh sentence. Your aim is recognising meaning quickly in context, not accumulating an unmanageable list.

Official practice material

Use the Goethe-Institut adult practice PDF for a full-length reading rehearsal and its official answer key. The short exercises here isolate reasoning skills; they do not reproduce the length, difficulty or coverage of a full exam. Keep a fresh official set for a timed attempt instead of treating a remembered score as progress.

After a full paper, classify each error as vocabulary, sentence structure, overlooked condition, wrong speaker or rushed transfer. Choose one category to work on before your next timed attempt. If you understood the text but repeatedly chose a distractor, practise explaining why each wrong option is wrong. If too many sentences were inaccessible, spend more time on level-appropriate reading before repeating exam papers.

Questions

Must I understand every word?

No. Identify the claim and supporting evidence. An unfamiliar adjective may be irrelevant; an unfamiliar negative or condition can change the answer. Decide which kind of gap you have before spending time on it.

Should I read the questions or the text first?

Use the questions to establish what you need, then read enough context to avoid isolated-word matching. For sentence insertion, read the whole text first so that you understand its progression.

Does a course replace official practice?

No. Language study develops the skills; official practice familiarises you with the provider’s instructions and full-length format. Use both, and seek feedback on recurring comprehension errors.
