Goethe C2 exam (GDS): format, tasks and preparation

The Goethe-Zertifikat C2: Großes Deutsches Sprachdiplom is the highest Goethe certificate. It proves near-native command of German, and it is one of the certificates that exempt you from further language proof at German universities. Here is what the four modules contain, how they differ from C1, and how to prepare.

Checked against the Goethe-Institut's C2 exam regulations (version of 1 September 2025) and its official C2 model test · Last checked 5 November 2026

In 30 seconds

  • Four modules: Lesen 80 min, Hören about 35 min, Schreiben 80 min, Sprechen about 15 min as an individual exam, after 15 minutes' preparation.
  • Each module is passed separately with 60 of 100 points, and can be taken and retaken individually.
  • Writing includes a precise rewriting task and a long text; two of the four topics are based on literature from a list published each year.
  • Under the national framework for university admission, a passed Goethe C2 exempts you from further German proof. Details →

Exam pattern

Goethe-Zertifikat C2: GDS
ModuleTimePartsFormat
Lesen80 min4Written, in a group
Hören≈ 35 min3Written, in a group
Schreiben80 min2Written, in a group
Sprechen≈ 15 min2Individual exam with the examiners, after 15 minutes' preparation

Each module is scored out of 100 and passed with 60. You receive a certificate for each module you pass, and later module results can be combined with earlier ones (how modules and certificates work).

Lesen: reading (80 minutes)

The official model test shows a working time for each part:

PartTimeTextTask
125 minA commentary10 gaps: choose the right word or phrase (4 options)
220 minA specialised articleMatch statements to sections; some statements match no section
325 minA reportage with paragraphs removedPut the paragraphs back; one does not fit
410 minSeveral job advertisementsSkim and match 8 statements to the right advertisement

Part 1 tests fine distinctions in vocabulary and collocation; Part 4 tests speed. Both reward years of wide reading more than last-minute strategy.

Hören: listening (about 35 minutes)

  • Part 1 (about 12 min): five short radio excerpts on different topics, heard once; decide whether statements match the content.
  • Part 2 (about 5 min): a conversation between two people, heard once; decide whether an opinion is held by one speaker or by both.
  • Part 3 (about 18 min): an interview with an expert, heard twice; 10 multiple-choice questions.

Two of the three parts are heard only once. Part 2 is the trap: speakers often agree in words but differ in nuance, or politely disagree while seeming to agree.

Schreiben: writing (80 minutes)

Part 1: rewriting (20 minutes)

You revise marked passages of a short presentation text, each time using a given word that must not be changed, and make all the other changes this requires. This is a test of grammatical flexibility: turning a verb into a noun phrase, an active sentence into a passive one, a clause into a participial construction.

The kind of transformation it asks for (our example)

Weil die Preise stark gestiegen sind, … → (Anstieg) Aufgrund des starken Anstiegs der Preise …

Part 2: a longer text (60 minutes)

You choose one of four topics: two general topics and two based on literature. In the official model test, a general topic asks for a detailed letter to the editor of about 350 words, responding to statements from a newspaper series. The book titles for the literature-based topics apply for one calendar year and are published by the Goethe-Institut online. Only choose a literature topic if you have read the book properly.

At this level, examiners expect a clearly structured, stylistically appropriate text: an engaging opening, each statement addressed with argument and example, and a conclusion that reads as a conclusion, not a repetition.

Sprechen: speaking (about 15 minutes, individual)

You have 15 minutes to prepare both parts.

  • Part 1, production (about 10 minutes): you choose one of two topics and give a talk of about 5 minutes, weighing different viewpoints with examples; short quotations on the task sheet can guide you. Then the examiners ask questions for about 2–3 minutes.
  • Part 2, interaction (about 5 minutes): you choose one of two topics, pick one of several statements, and open a discussion with the examiner, defending and nuancing your view.

C2 speaking rewards precision and register: Das trifft nur bedingt zu. · Ich würde das differenzierter sehen. · Darauf lässt sich entgegnen, dass …

C1 or C2?

AspectGoethe C1Goethe C2
University admission (national framework)Not on the list; some universities accept itExempts you from further proof
SpeakingIn pairs, talk + discussionIndividual, talk + discussion with the examiner
WritingForum post + messageRewriting task + long text (incl. literature options)

For most careers in Germany, C1 is ample. C2 makes sense when a university programme names it, when you want a single certificate that settles the language question for study, or when your work depends on writing German at a near-native level. See the C1 exam guide to compare.

How to prepare

  • Read German every day: quality newspapers, essays, long-form reportage. Note collocations and turns of phrase, not single words.
  • Train transformations for Part 1 of writing: verbal style ↔ nominal style, active ↔ passive, clause ↔ participle.
  • Write one long text a week under time pressure, and have it corrected by someone at C2 level.
  • Speak about abstract topics: practise 5-minute talks that weigh two sides, with examples.
  • Use the official C2 model test (PDF) and practice set (PDF) for timed rehearsals.

The Goethe-Institut's rough guideline for completing C2 is more than 1,000 teaching units of 45 minutes, counted from the beginning.
