Klarweg/German grammar/Alphabet and pronunciation
Free guide · A1 Exercises with answers

The German alphabet and pronunciation: letters, umlauts, ß and eight sound rules.

German is written almost exactly as it is spoken. Like Devanagari, once you know how each letter or letter group sounds, you can read words you have never seen. This guide gives you the 26 letters and their names, the umlauts ä, ö, ü and the ß, and eight sound rules that cover most beginner mistakes. Several German sounds already exist in Hindi: sch is श, j is य.

In 30 seconds

  • 26 letters as in English, plus ä, ö, ü and ß.
  • w sounds like English v; v usually like f; z like "ts"; j like y.
  • ei = "eye" (mein), ie = "ee" (Liebe). Read the second letter.
  • Most words are stressed on the first syllable.

The letters and their names

You need the letter names to spell your name, an email address or a street, which comes up in the first lesson and in the Goethe A1 speaking test. The pronunciation column uses English-style spelling.

The ones Indian learners mix up most: E (eh) vs I (ee), J (yot), V (fow) vs W (veh), and Z (tsett).

ä, ö, ü and ß

  • ä: like the "e" in "bed" (Käse, Männer).
  • ö: say "e" as in "bed" or "ay", then round your lips (schön, Köln).
  • ü: say "ee", then round your lips as for "oo" (für, München).
  • ß (Eszett): a sharp "ss", used after a long vowel or ei/au (Straße, heißen). After a short vowel, write ss (Wasser, muss). Switzerland always writes ss.

The umlaut changes the word: schon (already) vs schön (beautiful), Mutter (mother) vs Mütter (mothers). Without a German keyboard, write ae, oe, ue, ss: Muenchen, Strasse. That is also how umlauts appear in many passports and forms.

Two more patterns: s before a vowel sounds like English z in most of Germany (Sonne, Salz), and the ending -er sounds like a short "a" (Lehrer, Wasser). The h after a vowel is silent and only makes the vowel long (gehen, Uhr).

Word stress

Most German words are stressed on the first syllable: AR-beit, KIN-der, AN-rufen. Two common exceptions: verbs with be-, ge-, er-, ver-, zer-, ent-, emp- stress the syllable after the prefix (be-ZAH-len, ver-STE-hen; see the separable verbs guide), and many foreign words stress the end (Uni-ver-si-TÄT, Stu-DENT).

Spelling your name aloud

On the phone, Germans spell with a word for each letter: A wie Anton, B wie Berta. The official spelling table (DIN 5009) switched to city names in 2022 (A wie Aachen, B wie Berlin), but you will hear both. Useful phrases:

  • Wie schreibt man das?How do you spell that?
  • Können Sie das bitte buchstabieren?Could you spell that, please?
  • Mein Name ist Sharma: S, H, A, R, M, A.
  • Das schreibt man mit Doppel-S.With a double s.

Common mistakes

  1. Not Wasser with an English wBut "Vasser"German w = English v.
  2. Not Vater with an English vBut "Fater"v is usually f.
  3. Not Wien and Wein said the sameBut Wien = "Veen", Wein = "Vine"Read the second letter: ie = ee, ei = eye.
  4. Not Zug with an English zBut "Tsook"z = ts, and final g = k.
  5. Not Sport as in EnglishBut "Shport"sp at the start = shp.
  6. Not schon for "beautiful"But schönThe umlaut changes the meaning.

Exercises

Answer, then open the answer.

  1. Which means "wine": Wein or Wien?
    Show answer

    Wein ("vine"). Wien is Vienna.

  2. How does the w in Wohnung sound?
    Show answer

    Like English v.

  3. How does the v in Vogel sound?
    Show answer

    Like f: "Fogel".

  4. How does the z in Zimmer sound?
    Show answer

    ts: "Tsimmer". Hindi त्स.

  5. How does st in Stadt sound?
    Show answer

    sht: "Shtatt".

  6. Throaty or soft ch? Nacht
    Show answer

    Throaty (after a).

  7. Throaty or soft ch? nicht
    Show answer

    Soft (after i).

  8. What sound ends Freund?
    Show answer

    t: final d sounds like t.

  9. ß or ss? Stra__e, Wa__er
    Show answer

    Straße (long vowel), Wasser (short vowel).

  10. Write without umlauts: Düsseldorf, Größe
    Show answer

    Duesseldorf, Groesse

  11. Which syllable is stressed? verstehen
    Show answer

    The second: ver-STE-hen.

  12. Spell JUNG aloud, letter by letter.
    Show answer

    yot, oo, enn, geh
