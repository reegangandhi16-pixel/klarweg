Klarweg/German grammar/Genitiv

Genitiv — des, der and possession in German, made clear.

The Genitiv shows whose something is: das Auto meines Vaters. In everyday speech Germans often replace it, but in writing, on signs, in the news and after prepositions like wegen and trotz, you need it. Hindi's का, की, के do the same job, with one twist that catches many learners.

In 30 seconds

  • Articles: des (masculine, neuter), der (feminine, plural); eines/einer, meines/meiner.
  • Masculine and neuter nouns add -s or -es: des Vaters, des Kindes.
  • Names add -s, with no apostrophe, and come first: Rahuls Handy.
  • In speech, von + Dativ often replaces it: das Auto von meinem Vater.

का, की, के and the twist

Hindi marks possession with का, की, के: पिता की गाड़ी (father's car). German uses the Genitiv: das Auto des Vaters. Two differences matter:

  1. Order. In Hindi the owner comes first (पिता की गाड़ी). In German the owner usually comes second (das Auto des Vaters), except with names (Vaters Auto sounds old-fashioned, but Rahuls Auto is normal).
  2. Agreement. Hindi का/की/के agrees with the thing owned: पिता की गाड़ी because गाड़ी is feminine. German des/der agrees with the owner: das Auto des Vaters because der Vater is masculine. So die Tasche der Mutter, die Tasche des Vaters.

  • One-syllable masculine and neuter nouns often take -es (des Mannes, des Buches); longer ones take -s (des Computers, des Lehrers). Nouns ending in -s, -ß, -z need -es (des Hauses).
  • Some masculine nouns take -(e)n instead (n-declension): des Kollegen, des Studenten, des Menschen.
  • The question word is Wessen?: Wessen Tasche ist das?

Names: Rahuls Handy

With names, the Genitiv comes first and simply adds -s, with no apostrophe:

  • Rahuls Handy, Priyas Wohnung, Deutschlands HauptstadtSame order as Hindi: राहुल का फ़ोन.
  • Hans' FahrradNames ending in -s, -ß, -x, -z take only an apostrophe.

Prepositions with the Genitiv

Common Genitiv prepositions
PrepositionMeaningExample

währendduringWährend des Kurses lerne ich viel.

innerhalb / außerhalbwithin / outsideInnerhalb einer Woche

In everyday speech, many Germans use the Dativ after wegen and trotz (wegen dem Regen). In exams and formal writing, use the Genitiv.

Genitiv or von?

In conversation, von + Dativ often replaces the Genitiv:

  • das Auto meines Vaters → das Auto von meinem VaterBoth correct; the first is more formal.

For B1 and higher writing tasks, a few correct Genitiv forms show range. For speaking, von is natural.

Common mistakes

  1. Not das Auto der VaterBut das Auto des VatersThe article agrees with the owner (der Vater, masculine), and the noun gets -s.
  2. Not die Wohnung des SchwesterBut die Wohnung der SchwesterFeminine owner: der, no ending on the noun.
  3. Not Rahul's HandyBut Rahuls HandyNo apostrophe in German.
  4. Not des StudentBut des Studentenn-declension nouns take -en, not -s.
  5. Not Wegen das schlechte Wetter … (in writing)But Wegen des schlechten Wetters …Genitiv after wegen; the adjective takes -en (adjective endings).
