German numbers — einundzwanzig and beyond, made clear.

German numbers from 21 to 99 are said "backwards": einundzwanzig, one-and-twenty. English speakers find this strange. Hindi speakers don't, because इक्कीस also puts the one before the twenty. Here are the numbers, prices, years, phone numbers, and how lakh and crore translate.

In 30 seconds

  • 21–99: unit + und + ten, written as one word: einundzwanzig, siebenundvierzig.
  • Watch the spellings: sechzehn, siebzehn, dreißig, sechzig, siebzig.
  • German uses a comma for decimals and a dot for thousands: 3,50 €, 10.000.
  • 1 lakh = hunderttausend; 10 lakh = eine Million; 1 crore = zehn Millionen.

21 to 99: the Hindi advantage

From 21, German says the unit first, then und, then the ten, all as one word:

  • 21 — einundzwanzigone-and-twenty · इक्कीस (एक + बीस)
  • 35 — fünfunddreißigfive-and-thirty · पैंतीस (पाँच + तीस)
  • 99 — neunundneunzignine-and-ninety

Hindi numbers also put the unit before the ten, so the German order feels natural. The difference is that German is perfectly regular, while Hindi has a separate word for each number. Tens: zwanzig, dreißig, vierzig, fünfzig, sechzig, siebzig, achtzig, neunzig. Note dreißig with ß, and sechzig, siebzig, which shorten sechs and sieben. In compounds, eins becomes ein: einundzwanzig.

Compound numbers are written as one word up to a million: 345 = dreihundertfünfundvierzig, 2.026 = zweitausendsechsundzwanzig. And note the grouping: India writes 1,00,000; Germany writes 100.000.

Prices, years, phone numbers

  • 3,50 € — drei Euro fünfzigComma for decimals; say "Euro" in the middle.
  • 1998 — neunzehnhundertachtundneunzigYears before 2000: "nineteen hundred …".
  • 2026 — zweitausendsechsundzwanzigFrom 2000: "two thousand …". For a year, say im Jahr 2026 or just 2026, never "in 2026".
  • 0176 23 45 67 — null eins sieben sechs, dreiundzwanzig, fünfundvierzig, siebenundsechzigPhone numbers are often read in pairs; digit by digit is fine too.

First, second, third

Ordinals add -te up to 19 and -ste from 20, with three irregular ones: der erste, der zweite, der dritte, der vierte … der siebte, der achte … der zwanzigste. In writing, a dot marks an ordinal: am 5. November = am fünften November.

Common mistakes

  1. Not zwanzigeinsBut einundzwanzigUnit first, as in Hindi इक्कीस.
  2. Not sechszehn, siebenzehnBut sechzehn, siebzehnShortened forms.
  3. Not dreizigBut dreißigThe only ten with -ßig.
  4. Not 3.50 €But 3,50 €Comma for decimals; the dot separates thousands.
  5. Not Ich bin in 2001 geboren.But Ich bin 2001 geboren.No in before a bare year.
