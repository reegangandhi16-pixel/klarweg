Example lesson · A1 week 9Perfekt mit sein

01 · The teacher explains

  • IchSubject
  • binVerb · pos. 2
  • ins KinoPlace
  • gegangen.Verb · end

gehen means moving from one place to another. Verbs of movement form the Perfekt with sein, not haben.

The helper verb bin sits in position 2; the participle gegangen goes to the end.

gehen का मतलब है एक जगह से दूसरी जगह जाना। ऐसी गति वाली क्रियाओं का Perfekt haben से नहीं, sein से बनता है।

सहायक क्रिया bin दूसरे स्थान पर आती है और gegangen वाक्य के अंत में।

02 · Your turn

TeacherUnd du? Was hast du am Wochenende gemacht?

Ich ? nach Agra gefahren.

Choose

Richtig. fahren is movement to Agra, so the Perfekt takes sein: Ich bin nach Agra gefahren.fahren में Agra तक जाना है, इसलिए Perfekt sein से: Ich bin nach Agra gefahren.

Almost — this is the mistake most beginners make. In class, this is exactly the moment the teacher stops and explains.क्लास में ठीक इसी पल शिक्षक रुककर समझाते हैं।

03 · Corrected in the moment

Ich habe bin nach Agra gefahren.

Why: moving from A to B → sein. Everything else → usually haben. Now say the whole sentence again.क्यों: A से B तक जाना → sein। बाकी ज़्यादातर क्रियाएँ → haben। अब पूरा वाक्य फिर से बोलिए।

  • Ich habe Tee getrunken.No movement · haben
  • Ich bin nach Agra gefahren.Movement · sein

04 · Homework, with written feedback

  1. Am Samstag habe ich Cricket gespielt. ✓Good: the verb comes second after Am Samstag.बहुत अच्छा: Am Samstag के बाद क्रिया दूसरे स्थान पर है।
  2. Dann ich bin Dann bin ich nach Hause gegangen.After Dann, the verb still comes second.Dann के बाद भी क्रिया दूसरे स्थान पर ही आती है।
  3. Am Sonntag habe ich lange geschlafen. ✓schlafen is not movement, so haben is right.schlafen में कहीं जाना नहीं है, इसलिए haben सही है।

Teacher’s note: sein and haben all correct. Next: word order after time words.शिक्षक की टिप्पणी: sein और haben सब सही। अगला कदम: समय वाले शब्दों के बाद शब्द-क्रम।

01Inside a class

Ninety minutes, the same clear shape every time.

You always know what comes next: a quick warm-up, one point explained properly, then most of the time spent speaking, writing and being corrected.

0 min10253555708290

  1. Warm-up

    0–10 min

    Five or six quick questions on what you learned last class, last week and last month, so nothing is forgotten.

    Who talks: every learner answers at least once.

  2. Explanation

    10–25 min

    One grammar or speaking point, explained in English with Hindi comparisons where they help: the pattern, three examples and the one trap to avoid.

    Who talks: the teacher, checking you have understood.

  3. Model

    25–35 min

    The teacher uses the new German in a short dialogue or builds a sentence out loud; you notice the pattern and say it back.

    Who talks: the teacher, then the whole group together.

  4. Guided practice

    35–55 min

    Gap-fills out loud, question chains, pair practice and a listening task, from controlled to more open.

    Who talks: you, in pairs or in turn.

  5. Your turn

    55–70 min

    You create your own German: a role play, a short talk or a few written sentences.

    Who talks: you.

  6. Correction

    70–82 min

    A few anonymised mistakes from the class go on screen. The group corrects them, then the teacher names the rule.

    Who talks: the group, then the teacher.

  7. Wrap-up

    82–90 min

    A short check that the point has landed, the homework, and a look at the next class.

    Who talks: everyone.
