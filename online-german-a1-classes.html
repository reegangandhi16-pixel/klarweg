What an A1 course covers

By the end of A1 you should be able to introduce yourself, ask and answer simple questions, understand times, prices and short notices, fill in a form and write a short message. Grammar includes present-tense verbs, sein and haben, articles, kein and nicht, the accusative, modal verbs and word order. See the A1 syllabus checklist for the full list.

Try a first-class introduction

Most A1 classes start with introductions. Say your name and city out loud. Then ask a classmate where they live. Use the Hindi as a cue if you forget the next line.

See a model with Hindi cues

Ich heiße Asha. Ich wohne in Delhi. Wo wohnst du?

मेरा नाम आशा है। मैं दिल्ली में रहती हूँ। तुम कहाँ रहते हो?

Change both personal details, then say the three lines without reading. When you speak to a teacher or a stranger, use the formal question: Wo wohnen Sie?

Get the most from a ₹100 demo

The demo lasts 20 minutes on Google Meet, on a Friday or Sunday after 4 p.m. IST. You get a short level check, a sample of the teaching style and a course recommendation. You can move a confirmed demo once, free of charge, up to 12 hours before it starts.

Take one question with you: “I’m starting from zero and can study on Tuesday and Saturday evenings IST. Which format fits me, and when does the next A1 batch start?” After the demo, check whether you can repeat one correction on your own. That tells you more about the teaching than the fee does.
