About the free practice library

How these exercises are made

Klarweg's free practice notes combine English guidance with German examples. Each topic includes four explained gap-fill questions and a separate writing task with one possible answer.

The writing tasks ask you to use a pattern in a new situation. They are self-check activities: a different natural sentence can be correct, and the model is not an automatic score. Level labels describe the intended practice, not certification or an official Goethe assessment.

Preparation includes AI assistance, editorial checks of the wording and automated checks of answer banks, repeated text and links. Independent native German linguistic and pedagogic review has not yet been completed.

Found a problem? Contact Klarweg with the lesson URL and question number so the relevant source can be checked and corrected.

For Hindi speakers

The sounds that trip Hindi speakers up.

Hindi has no clean v/w split and no German ch. These pairs are the ones worth drilling first.

Pair 01 · v vs w
Vater
/ˈfaːtɐ/
faatar · father
Wasser
/ˈvasɐ/
vaasar · water

German v sounds like English f; German w sounds like English v.

Pair 02 · ich vs Buch
ich
/ɪç/
ish · soft hiss
Buch
/buːx/
bookh · throat

After i/e → soft ç. After a/o/u → hard x (like Hindi kh).

Pair 03 · ö & ü
schön
/ʃøːn/
round lips, say e
müde
/ˈmyːdə/
round lips, say ee

No Hindi equivalent — shape lips for o/u, but say e/ee inside.
