n-Deklination: why it's den Studenten and Herrn Müller.

A small group of masculine nouns adds -n or -en in every case except the Nominativ: der Student, but den Studenten, dem Studenten. It matters in every formal letter (an Herrn Müller) and in the B1 exam. The good news: these nouns fall into a few recognisable groups, and Hindi speakers already know the idea from लड़का → लड़के को.

In 30 seconds

  • Only masculine nouns, mostly people and animals.
  • Nominativ singular has no ending. Every other form, singular and plural, ends in -(e)n: Ich kenne den Kollegen.
  • Three groups: masculine nouns in -e (Junge, Kunde), foreign words in -ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -oge (Student, Tourist), and a short list (Herr, Mensch, Nachbar).
  • Herr → Herrn in the singular, Herren in the plural.
  • Nominativ
  • Akkusativ
  • Dativ

The pattern

Compare an n-noun with an ordinary masculine noun. The article changes for both; only the n-noun changes its ending as well.

der Student (n-noun) vs der Lehrer (ordinary)
Casen-nounOrdinary
Nominativder Studentder Lehrer
Akkusativden Studentenden Lehrer
Dativdem Studentendem Lehrer
Genitivdes Studentendes Lehrers
Pluraldie Studentendie Lehrer

Notice the Genitiv: n-nouns take -en, not the usual -s. For the cases themselves, see the Akkusativ, Dativ and Genitiv guides.

  • Ich frage den Praktikanten.I'm asking the intern.
  • Das Paket ist für den Nachbarn.The parcel is for the neighbour.
  • Sie spricht mit dem Kunden.She is talking to the customer.

Which nouns? Three groups

Recognising n-nouns
GroupExamples
Masculine, ending in -eder Junge, der Kollege, der Kunde, der Neffe, der Löwe, der Affe, der Franzose, der Experte
Foreign endingsder Student, der Präsident, der Praktikant, der Polizist, der Tourist, der Soldat, der Kandidat, der Biologe, der Fotograf
Learn by heartder Herr, der Mensch, der Nachbar, der Bauer, der Held, der Bär, der Prinz

The foreign endings are -ent, -ant, -ist, -at, -oge and -graf. Masculine nouns in -e that refer to people are almost always n-nouns: der Franzose is one, while der Inder, without the -e, is ordinary. Feminine and neuter nouns never belong to this group (the one neuter exception, das Herz, is below).

Herr and formal letters

Herr adds -n in the singular and -en in the plural. This shows up in letters and on envelopes:

  • Sehr geehrter Herr Müller, …The greeting is Nominativ: no ending.
  • An Herrn MüllerThe address line: an + Akkusativ.
  • Ich habe mit Herrn Müller gesprochen.
  • Sehr geehrte Damen und Herren, …Plural.

The formal letter in the B1 and B2 writing exams is a good place to show this correctly; see the B1 Schreiben guide.

Name, Gedanke and the -ns Genitiv

A few abstract nouns follow the pattern but add -ns in the Genitiv: der Name, den Namen, dem Namen, des Namens. Others like it are der Gedanke, der Glaube, der Buchstabe. The neuter das Herz is similar: dem Herzen, des Herzens.

  • Wie schreibt man den Anfang des Namens?
  • Ich habe den Namen vergessen.

In speech and in exams

In casual speech you may hear the ending dropped (Ich habe den Student gesehen). In writing, and in the Goethe exams, use the ending: it is part of correct grammar and easy to check.

Hindi लड़का and लड़के

A noun that changes outside its base form
HindiGerman
लड़का खेल रहा है।Der Junge spielt.
मैंने लड़के को देखा।Ich habe den Jungen gesehen.
मैं लड़के से बात कर रहा हूँ।Ich spreche mit dem Jungen.

In Hindi, लड़का becomes लड़के as soon as को, से or another postposition follows. German n-nouns behave the same way: one base form for the subject, a changed form everywhere else. The difference is that in German only these few masculine nouns do it.

Common mistakes

  1. Not Ich frage den Student.But Ich frage den Studenten.Akkusativ of an n-noun: -en.
  2. Not An Herr MeierBut An Herrn Meieran + Akkusativ: Herrn.
  3. Not Sehr geehrter Herrn Meier,But Sehr geehrter Herr Meier,The greeting is Nominativ.
  4. Not Ich spreche mit dem Kollege.But Ich spreche mit dem Kollegen.Dativ of an n-noun: -n.
  5. Not das Auto des NachbarsBut das Auto des Nachbarnn-nouns take -n in the Genitiv, not -s.
  6. Not Ich besuche den Lehrern.But Ich besuche den Lehrer.Lehrer is an ordinary noun: no extra ending.
