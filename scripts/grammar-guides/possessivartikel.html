German possessive articles — mein, dein, sein, ihr, made clear.

My, your, his, her: German possessives make two decisions at once. The word itself depends on the owner; the ending depends on the thing owned. Hindi's अपना works differently, and that difference explains the most common mistake Hindi speakers make with sein and ihr.

In 30 seconds

  • Owner → word: mein, dein, sein (his/its), ihr (her), unser, euer, ihr (their), Ihr (your, polite).
  • Thing owned → ending, exactly like ein/kein: mein Bruder, meine Schwester, mein Kind, meine Eltern.
  • sein or ihr depends on whether the owner is masculine/neuter or feminine: Rahul und seine Mutter, Priya und ihre Mutter.
  • euer loses an e with endings: eure Wohnung.

sein or ihr? The Hindi अपना trap

In Hindi, the possessive agrees with the thing owned: राहुल अपनी माँ से मिलता है and प्रिया अपनी माँ से मिलती है both use अपनी, because माँ is feminine. German first asks who the owner is:

  • Rahul besucht seine Mutter.Rahul is male → sein-; Mutter is feminine → -e.
  • Priya besucht ihre Mutter.Priya is female → ihr-; Mutter is feminine → -e.
  • Priya besucht ihren Vater.Owner female → ihr-; Vater is masculine, Akkusativ → -en.

So the stem (sein/ihr) follows the owner, like English "his/her", and only the ending follows the thing owned, like Hindi अपना/अपनी.

Choose the ending: what is owned?

Possessives take the same endings as ein and kein:

Genmeinesmeinermeinesmeiner

The same endings go on every possessive: deinen, seinem, ihrer, unsere. See the Akkusativ and Dativ guides for when each case is used.

euer and Ihr

  • euer drops its second e when it takes an ending: euer Sohn, but eure Tochter, euren Sohn.
  • unser keeps it: unsere Wohnung, unseren Lehrer.
  • The polite Ihr (your) is always written with a capital I, like Sie: Wie ist Ihr Name? Lower-case ihr means "her" or "their".

Common mistakes

  1. Not Priya besucht seine Mutter. (meaning her own mother)But Priya besucht ihre Mutter.The owner is female → ihr-.
  2. Not Rahul ruft ihre Schwester an. (meaning his sister)But Rahul ruft seine Schwester an.The owner is male → sein-, even though Schwester is feminine.
  3. Not Ich habe mein Bruder angerufen.But Ich habe meinen Bruder angerufen.Masculine Akkusativ: -en.
  4. Not euere WohnungBut eure Wohnungeuer drops the e before an ending.
  5. Not Wie ist ihr Name? (to a stranger)But Wie ist Ihr Name?The polite "your" has a capital I.
